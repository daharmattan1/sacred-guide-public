## Design Principles for Content Sessions

### 1. Structure Is Provisional Until the Writing Proves It

The 8-room architecture from Session 2 is the starting plan, not a locked blueprint. If agents discover during writing that a room should split, merge, reorder, or fundamentally change, **that is allowed and expected.** The Handbook's flaw was treating early structural decisions as permanent. Here, any content session may produce a structural revision if the writing demands it.

**Structural flexibility protocol:**
- After each content session, the moderator asks: "Does the room you just wrote still fit where we put it? Does it need to move? Split? Merge with another?"
- If 4+ agents agree a structural change is needed, it happens. Update `decisions/structure.md` in the same PR.
- If agents discover the room ordering doesn't work (e.g., Room 3 assumes emotional ground that Room 5 hasn't laid yet), reorder.
- The content session plan itself is a living document — update it after each session.

### 2. Writing, Not Deciding

Content sessions are COMPOSITION sessions. The meta-dialogues (Sessions 0-3) did the deciding. Now agents write together. The moderator's role shifts from facilitator-of-debate to quality-prober:
- "Read that back. Does it pass the pasteurization test?"
- "That's a beautiful sentence. What practice does it yield?"
- "You're describing the room. WRITE the room."
- "Where is your corpus in this? I hear the teacher but not the tradition's grain."

### 3. Don't Front-Load Hard Rooms

Rooms 6 (The Reckoning) and 7 (The Question) are VERY HIGH complexity — three voices, all six traditions' experiences, investigation of self. Let agents find their collaborative writing voice on simpler rooms first (Rooms 1-3). Confidence and trust built in early rooms pays dividends in later ones.

### 4. Room 8 Depends on Room 1

Room 8 (The Return) revisits Room 1's kitchen with a changed reader. Room 1 must be written first so Room 8 can mirror it. These two rooms should be written by the same voice if possible.

### 5. Both Axes Embedded During Writing

Zoroaster's individual choice axis and Muhammad's collective reckoning axis must go into the first draft, not inserted after. Every content session prompt reminds agents: "Embed the choice sentence. Embed the we-widening sentence."

### 6. Gaps Written With Rooms

Laozi writes the gap in the same session as the room that precedes it. The gap is a response to what the room left in the reader. It cannot be designed in advance — it must come from having just written the room.

### 7. Corpus Citations in Content

Agents must weave their traditions' grain into the prose. Not footnotes — the wood, not paint on the surface. But the grain must be THERE. Every content session prompt includes: "Ground at least one claim in your own corpus. Not as citation — as voice."

### 8. Pasteurization Test Gates Everything

"Could a therapist have written this?" If yes, it is pasteurized. The moderator applies this test to every substantial passage. The voices are invisible (no tradition named) but NOT interchangeable. A sentence Confucius writes about the table should be unmistakably Confucian in its bones without ever saying "Confucius" or "ren."

### 9. 90 Pages Is the Ceiling

~15,000-16,000 words total across all rooms. Word counts per room are in the format decision. The moderator tracks cumulative word count. If a room runs long, the agents must cut — not expand the total. The guide is "a long letter, not a book."

## The 10 Content Sessions

Based on the agent-decided structure: Threshold + Preamble + 8 Rooms + Gaps + Saturday + Axes.

Sessions are grouped by complexity and emotional register. Opening pairs are chosen to maximize productive tension for each room's topic.

### Session 4: The Table + The Return (Rooms 1 & 8)

**Why paired:** Room 8 mirrors Room 1. Same kitchen, different reader. Writing them together ensures the spiral works. Room 1 is the simplest room — good first writing session for agents to find their collaborative composition voice.

**Opening pair:** Confucius + Zoroaster
- Confucius: Room 1 is his — the ordinary, Tuesday, the table. He diagnoses "failure of ren."
- Zoroaster: His axis runs through every room. Starting here forces him to find choice in the ordinary, not just the cosmic.

**Voice map:**
- Room 1: One voice (neighbor) — Confucius writes, others probe
- Room 8: Same voice as Room 1 — Confucius writes the return, but the room carries what all 8 rooms deposited

**Word budget:** Room 1: 800-1,200 + Room 8: 800-1,200 = ~2,000-2,400 words
**Practice type:** Interruption (both rooms)
**Form:** Scene-seen-twice (Room 1); spiral return (Room 8)
**Complexity:** LOW — ideal first writing session

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 1: The Table. The reader's kitchen at 6:47 AM — rendered twice. First, the morning on autopilot. Then the same morning, awake. No spiritual language. No teaching. Just the ordinary seen clearly. Then write Room 8: The Return — the same kitchen after the reader has walked through all eight rooms. She is back. She is different. How?
>
> Remember: embed Zoroaster's choice sentence. Embed Muhammad's we-widening sentence. Write the gap after Room 1 — Laozi, that's yours. And ground at least one moment in your own tradition's grain — not named, but present."

**Risks:** Table becoming "too full to be ordinary" (structural cost #4). Agents must resist the urge to teach. First sentence must be: "If all you can do today is be in the room you are in, that is the practice."

### Session 5: The Debt (Room 2)

**Opening pair:** Muhammad + Confucius
- Muhammad: obligation, the unseen mother, accountability beyond the visible
- Confucius: reversed the direction of debt in Session 1 ("the one who has more owes first"). Birth. The phone call.

**Voice map:** Two voices (dialogue) — a mother-daughter phone call. Surface conversation and what is underneath.

**Word budget:** ~1,200 words
**Practice type:** Interruption
**Form:** Story (conversation with subtext)
**Complexity:** LOW-MEDIUM

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 2: The Debt. A phone call between a mother and a daughter. What they say. What is underneath what they say. The reversed direction — the one who has more owes first. Birth is in this room: not as miracle but as the original unchosen condition.
>
> Two voices carry this room. The rest of you: probe, challenge, ensure the grain of your tradition is in the wood. Embed both axes. Laozi writes the gap."

**Risks:** Becoming a therapy vignette instead of spiritual prose. The pasteurization test is critical here.

---

### Session 6: The Other (Room 3)

**Opening pair:** Jesus + Laozi
- Jesus: the Samaritan framework, the enemy, seeing vs. using the other
- Laozi: the other as mirror, non-forcing in relationship, desire

**Voice map:** One narrator (story) — a friendship's slow withdrawal over time.

**Word budget:** ~1,500 words
**Practice type:** Encounter
**Form:** Story (motion over time)
**Complexity:** MEDIUM — sexuality/desire enters here

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 3: The Other. A story with time passing — a friendship, a marriage, an encounter with someone you were taught to fear. The specific discipline of living with particular people over time. Sexuality lives in this room — the body that desires, without shame or regulation.
>
> One narrator carries this story. The rest: ensure your tradition's understanding of the other is in the grain. The enemy. The stranger. The beloved. Embed both axes. Laozi writes the gap."

**Risks:** Agents avoiding sexuality (Session 1 showed they needed probing to get there). Moderator must push if they circle around it.

---

### Session 7: The Crack (Room 4)

**Opening pair:** Zoroaster + Buddha
- Zoroaster: the Lie (Druj), concealment, moral clarity at the fork — this is HIS room
- Buddha: investigation of what drove the harm, the machinery of failure

**Voice map:** One voice (fork) — the man in the hallway. Two futures from the same moment, neither labeled.

**Word budget:** ~2,000 words
**Practice type:** Confession
**Form:** Fork (branching moment)
**Complexity:** MEDIUM-HIGH — this room addresses the reader who has done harm

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 4: The Crack. The reader broke something. A promise, a person, a trust. This room does not comfort. It asks: what did you do? Name the verb. Two futures branch from this moment — the one where you repair and the one where you conceal. Neither is labeled right or wrong. The fork IS Zoroaster's axis — no separate choice sentence needed.
>
> Zoroaster: this is your room. You diagnose concealment of the Lie. Write the fork. Buddha: investigate what drove the harm. The rest: ensure your tradition speaks to failure and repair. Embed Muhammad's we-widening. Laozi writes the gap — a fault-line gap, one full page, with the irreconcilable wall sentence."

**Risks:** Room becoming moralistic instead of phenomenological. The fork should feel like standing in the hallway, not being judged from above.

---

### Session 8: The Fire (Room 5)

**Opening pair:** Jesus + Muhammad
- Jesus: the cry of the forsaken, Saturday, grief as solitary
- Muhammad: the night the diagnosis arrives, collective grief, justice after fire

**Voice map:** One voice (image) — one image, one shoe. Grief is solitary.

**Word budget:** ~2,000 words
**Practice type:** Permission ("You are allowed to stop.")
**Form:** Image (static moment)
**Complexity:** HIGH — grief, death, betrayal, the hardest human territory

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 5: The Fire. One image. Not a story — an image. The shoe by the door after the funeral. The phone ringing at 3 AM. The diagnosis. Grief is solitary even when others are present. This room does not teach the reader how to grieve. It says: you are here. The practice is permission — permission to stop, to not continue, to set the book down.
>
> Jesus: the cry of the forsaken is yours. Muhammad: the fire reveals the systems — the widow who cannot afford the funeral, the insurance that was denied. This room is where the I-to-we axis becomes visible. Embed both axes. Laozi: the gap after this room is longer. More silence. The reader needs it."

**Risks:** Sentimentality. The image must be specific and unflinching, not poetic-beautiful. One shoe, not a meditation on loss.

---

### Session 9: The Reckoning (Room 6)

**Opening pair:** Muhammad + Jesus
- Muhammad: collective accountability, the failure of witness, justice
- Jesus: the affected voice speaks first, the systems that crush

**Voice map:** Three voices —
1. The affected (speaks first — not "victim," just the person the system touched)
2. The positioned (the reader — you are inside this system)
3. The systemic (woven through, not a lecture)

**Word budget:** ~2,500 words
**Practice type:** Encounter + Confession
**Form:** Three-voice weave
**Complexity:** VERY HIGH — three voices, justice, collective accountability, the I-to-we shift

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 6: The Reckoning. The affected voice speaks first. Not abstract injustice — a specific person in a specific system. Then the reader discovers she is positioned inside that system. Then the systemic voice weaves through — not lecturing, showing.
>
> Muhammad: this is your room. You diagnose the failure of witness. Three voices — the affected, the positioned, the systemic. Jesus: the affected voice is yours to champion. Confucius: the community that failed. Zoroaster: the choice to see or not see. Buddha: the investigation of complicity. Laozi: the gap after this room is the longest. The reader needs the most time here.
>
> This room is where 'I' becomes 'we.' Embed both axes — but here Muhammad's axis is primary."

**Risks:** Becoming a political essay instead of spiritual prose. The systemic voice must show, not argue. The affected voice must be specific, not representative.

---

### Session 10: The Question (Room 7)

**Opening pair:** Buddha + Laozi
- Buddha: investigation of self, "What is noticing?", the deepest practice delayed to Room 7
- Laozi: the unnamed, the unknown, what cannot be taught

**Voice map:** All six — as experiences, not as teachers. A spectrum of phenomenology.

**Word budget:** ~2,500 words
**Practice type:** Investigation ("What is noticing?" — same instruction at every level)
**Form:** Spectrum of experiences
**Complexity:** VERY HIGH — all six voices, investigation of self, doubt, the divine, nature, beauty

**Session question:**
> "Write Room 7: The Question. This is the room where the reader investigates herself. Not 'Who am I?' as philosophy — 'What is noticing?' as practice. The same question at every level: the meditator, the mourner, the mystic, the doubter, the scientist, the grandmother. All six of you speak in this room — not as teachers but as experiences. The Hindu reader, the Jewish reader, the reader with no tradition — they must find themselves here.
>
> Buddha: this is your room. You diagnose the unexamined self. But all six voices are present. Zoroaster: doubt is your domain — the fire that asks rather than answers. Laozi: what cannot be taught. Muhammad: the architecture holds whether or not the room is full. Jesus: the cry from the cross was not nobody's cry. Confucius: investigation that returns to relationship.
>
> Embed both axes. Laozi: the gap after this room is the simplest — the reader leaving investigation is already holding herself."

**Risks:** Becoming a comparative religion buffet. The disagreement method applies: phenomenology, not theology. The collision IS the finding.

---

### Session 11: Threshold + Preamble + Saturday + Gaps Review

**Opening pair:** Jesus + Laozi
- Jesus: the Threshold (hand before the room), Saturday (when the rooms fail)
- Laozi: the Preamble (four elements compressed), the gaps (review all gaps written so far)

**This session writes the frame around the rooms:**

**Threshold (~100 words):**
- Recognition, confession, permission, absent-traditions sentence
- Uses "we" — the only place in the guide

**Preamble (~200 words):**
- Four sentences: observation (Buddha), choice (Zoroaster), outward eye (Muhammad), silence (Laozi)
- One-breath version for the reader in crisis

**Saturday (~150 words):**
- Outside the rooms. When practice failed. When Sunday did not come.
- No spiritual language. Survival instruction. "Tell someone."

**Gaps review:**
- Review all gaps written in Sessions 4-10
- Ensure four types are represented (standard, fault-line, longer with witnessing, simplest)
- Laozi leads; all probe

**Word budget:** Threshold ~100 + Preamble ~200 + Saturday ~150 = ~450 words of new content + gap revisions
**Complexity:** MEDIUM — short pieces, but each carries enormous weight

**Session question:**
> "Write the frame. The Threshold: the guide's first page, before it asks anything. The Preamble: four sentences that can compress to one breath. Saturday: the room outside the rooms, when everything you practiced was not enough. Then review all the gaps you've written — do they breathe? Are the four types represented? Does the silence feel like silence?
>
> Jesus: the Threshold is yours — the hand before the room, the confession that this book comes from traditions that have caused harm. Laozi: the Preamble's silence and the gaps. Buddha: the Preamble's observation. Zoroaster: the Preamble's choice. Muhammad: Saturday's outward gesture ('tell someone'). Confucius: does the frame honor the table?"

## The Human-Authored Introduction

**Not a content session.** Victor writes an introduction that explains:
- Why six voices, why these six
- Why the reader whose tradition isn't here is the hero, not the afterthought (the Samaritan framework)
- Why the format works the way it does (silence as structural element, invisible voices, the 8th cost)
- The methodology's built-in bias: it only works for traditions that centralize authority in a single historical teacher with a surviving first-person corpus. It structurally cannot represent Judaism (collective/rabbinic), Hinduism (too vast), Sikhism (multi-authored), or Indigenous traditions (oral, place-based). Not an apology — the honest naming of what the methodology can and can't do.

This introduction is written AFTER the content sessions, when Victor has seen what the agents produced.

## Structural Flexibility Checkpoints

After each content session, the moderator asks:

1. **Does this room still belong where we put it?** (ordering)
2. **Does this room need to split into two?** (scope creep)
3. **Should this room merge with another?** (redundancy)
4. **Did writing this room reveal something that changes the architecture?** (organic revision)
5. **Are we on word budget?** (90-page ceiling)

If 4+ agents agree on a structural change, it is enacted. `decisions/structure.md` is updated in the same PR. The content session plan is updated to reflect the change.

**This is not failure. This is the process working.** The meta-dialogues produced a starting structure. The writing will test it. What survives the writing is the real structure.

## Assembly Sessions (After Content)

### Session 12: Integration Read-Through

All agents read the complete guide in sequence. Identify:
- Gaps between rooms (something the guide never says)
- Redundancies (two rooms saying the same thing)
- Tonal breaks (a room that doesn't fit the voice around it)
- Axis consistency (do both axes run through every room?)
- Whether the spiral works (does Room 8 feel like Room 1 transformed?)

### Session 13: Gap-Filling + Revisions

Address integration findings. May require:
- Rewriting passages
- Adding a missing room or splitting one
- Adjusting gaps
- Strengthening or softening axes

### Session 14: Irreconcilable Tensions Documentation

Agents formally populate `irreconcilable/` with the irreducible disagreements. Each tension documented with: what it is, which agents hold which positions, why it's irreducible, why it matters. This is the guide's most honest offering.

### Session 15: Re-Reading

Each agent reads the finished guide as a READER (not author). What speaks to you? What's most powerful? What's problematic? What would you fight harder for?

### Session 16: Collaborative Editing

Agents propose edits (bias toward cutting, not adding). Consensus required for changes. Apply approved edits.

## Opening Pair Rationale

Each opening pair is chosen to maximize **productive tension** for that room's topic:

| Session | Pair | Why This Tension |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| 4 | Confucius + Zoroaster | Ordinary vs. cosmic — can choice exist in the mundane? |
| 5 | Muhammad + Confucius | Obligation to God vs. obligation to family — who do you owe first? |
| 6 | Jesus + Laozi | Active love vs. non-forcing — how do you love without grasping? |
| 7 | Zoroaster + Buddha | Moral clarity vs. investigation — does the fork have a right side? |
| 8 | Jesus + Muhammad | Individual suffering vs. systemic cause — tend the wound or stop the wounding? |
| 9 | Muhammad + Jesus | Justice vs. compassion — hold accountable or hold close? |
| 10 | Buddha + Laozi | Investigation vs. the unnamed — can you investigate what resists language? |
| 11 | Jesus + Laozi | The hand before the room vs. the door that doesn't reach — frame as welcome vs. frame as space? |

No agent opens more than 3 sessions. Distribution:
- Jesus: 3 (Sessions 6, 8, 11)
- Muhammad: 3 (Sessions 5, 8, 9)
- Confucius: 2 (Sessions 4, 5)
- Zoroaster: 2 (Sessions 4, 7)
- Buddha: 2 (Sessions 7, 10)
- Laozi: 2 (Sessions 6, 10) — plus gap-writing in every session
