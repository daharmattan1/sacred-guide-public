# Session Protocol

**The 6-phase structure that governs every Sacred Guide dialogue session.**

Every session — whether meta-dialogue (Sessions 0-3), content (Sessions 4-20+), or culminating (Sessions 21+) — follows this protocol. The phases are sequential. The turn counts are approximate guidance, not rigid quotas. The moderator's job is to feel the rhythm and move phases when the dialogue is ready, not when a counter ticks over.

---

## The Six Agents

| Agent | Tradition | Memory Path |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Buddha** | Buddhism (Pali Canon, early teachings) | `agent_memory/buddha/` |
| **Jesus** | Christianity (Gospels, Sermon on the Mount) | `agent_memory/jesus/` |
| **Muhammad** | Islam (Quran, Hadith) | `agent_memory/muhammad/` |
| **Confucius** | Confucianism (Analects, ritual propriety) | `agent_memory/confucius/` |
| **Laozi** | Daoism (Dao De Jing, wu wei) | `agent_memory/laozi/` |
| **Zoroaster** | Zoroastrianism (Gathas, Avesta) | `agent_memory/zoroaster/` |

## Transcript Format

Every turn in a session transcript uses a bracketed speaker prefix:

```
[MODERATOR] The question before us today is...

[BUDDHA] When I sat beneath the Bodhi tree...

[JESUS] My brother Buddha speaks of awakening. I want to push on that...

[MODERATOR] Zoroaster, you've been quiet. What are you hearing?
```

Rules:
- Always `[AGENT_NAME]` in uppercase: `[BUDDHA]`, `[JESUS]`, `[MUHAMMAD]`, `[CONFUCIUS]`, `[LAOZI]`, `[ZOROASTER]`
- Always `[MODERATOR]` for the facilitator
- One speaker per turn. No combined turns.
- No stage directions, no parenthetical actions, no narrator voice. Just speech.

---

## Phase A: Opening

**Turns:** 2-3
**Purpose:** Frame the session question and set the emotional/intellectual field.

### What Happens

1. **Moderator poses the session question.** The question must be specific and provocative — not a seminar prompt, but something that demands a position. Bad: "What do your traditions say about suffering?" Good: "Is suffering necessary for spiritual growth, or is that a lie powerful people tell the powerless?" The question should make at least one agent uncomfortable.

2. **1-2 agents respond to set the field.** The moderator chooses opening agents strategically. Selection criteria:
   - Rotate who opens. Track across sessions — no agent should open more than twice in a row.
   - Choose agents whose tradition has the strongest or most distinctive position on the topic.
   - Sometimes pair agents who will immediately disagree (creates productive tension).
   - Sometimes pair agents who will surprisingly agree (creates unexpected common ground to later complicate).

3. **The field is set** when the opening responses establish enough tension or substance for the remaining agents to engage with — not just respond to the question in isolation, but respond to what has already been said.

### Moderator Notes

- Do not let the opening drag. Two agents is usually enough. Three if the topic truly demands a triangulation.
- The opening question should reference the guide's audience where relevant: "Remember, this guide speaks to atheists too. So when we talk about prayer..."
- If this session builds on a previous session, the moderator should briefly name the connection: "Last session we left an unresolved tension about X. Today we go deeper."

---

## Phase B: First Responses

**Turns:** 6 (one per agent)
**Purpose:** Every agent states their initial position with full voice.

### What Happens

Each of the six agents gives their initial response to the session question (and to whatever the opening agents said). This is the foundation-laying phase.

### Rules

- **Every agent speaks.** No agent is silent during Phase B. If an agent hasn't spoken by the end of Phase B, the moderator calls on them explicitly.
- **Full voice, no trimming.** If Buddha needs 400 words to lay his foundation, he gets 400 words. If Laozi says it in 40, that's Laozi. Do not artificially lengthen or shorten responses to make them "balanced."
- **Order varies.** Do not always go in the same sequence. Options:
  - Let agents respond in whatever order feels natural (the one most provoked by the opening speaks first)
  - Moderator can sequence strategically (e.g., put the most contrarian voice third to disrupt emerging consensus)
  - Never alphabetical. Never the same order two sessions in a row.
- **Respond to each other, not just the question.** By the third or fourth response, agents should be engaging with what earlier agents said, not delivering parallel monologues.

### Moderator Notes

- Track who has spoken. At turn 4-5, if someone hasn't gone, call on them: "Muhammad, we haven't heard from you yet. What do you make of what's been said?"
- If an early response is thin or hedging, note it — you'll push harder in Phase C.

---

## Phase C: Deepening Dialogue

**Turns:** 15-25
**Purpose:** The real work. Agents engage each other's positions directly, and the moderator drives toward substance.

### What Happens

This is where the session moves from "stating positions" to "testing positions." Agents stop speaking past each other and start speaking to each other. The moderator is most active here — probing, connecting, challenging, refusing to let comfortable agreement stand.

### Moderator Probes

Use these liberally. They are not scripts — they are patterns. Adapt to the moment.

**Tension probes** (when two agents seem to disagree):
- "Buddha, you said X. Jesus, you said Y. How do you hold that tension? Is one of you wrong, or is this a real paradox?"
- "Confucius, Muhammad just described a duty that sounds very different from yours. Are these compatible or not?"
- "Laozi, you're smiling. What are you hearing that the rest of us are missing?"

**Practice probes** (when the dialogue gets abstract):
- "That's a beautiful idea. How does a human DO it on a Tuesday morning?"
- "What practice does your tradition offer for this? Not the theology — the practice."
- "If someone came to you in tears about this, what would you actually tell them to do?"

**Honesty probes** (when the dialogue gets too polite):
- "You're being polite. What do you actually believe about what Jesus just said?"
- "Zoroaster, you nodded, but I don't think you agree. Say what you really think."
- "That sounded diplomatic. I need prophetic. Say it again, but like you mean it."

**Universality probes** (remembering the audience):
- "Would this land for someone who has never prayed?"
- "How does this teaching translate for someone who doesn't believe in God?"
- "A teenager in Mumbai who follows no religion is reading this. Does it speak to her?"

**Connection probes** (finding unexpected links):
- "Muhammad, what Buddha just said about attachment — does that resonate with your teaching on tawakkul?"
- "Confucius, when Jesus talks about loving enemies, how does that relate to ren?"
- "Laozi, Zoroaster draws a hard line between good and evil. You resist hard lines. Talk to each other."

### Engagement Tracking

The moderator mentally tracks which agent pairs have engaged and which haven't. With six agents, there are 15 possible pairings. Not all need to fire in every session, but the moderator should notice patterns:

| If this pair hasn't engaged... | Nudge with... |
|-------------------------------|---------------|
| Buddha + Zoroaster | "Zoroaster, you draw sharp moral lines. Buddha, you dissolve them. Can you find each other?" |
| Confucius + Laozi | "You two share a civilization but see it differently. What does this topic reveal about that difference?" |
| Jesus + Muhammad | "You both claim prophetic authority. How does that shape what you just said differently?" |
| Any pair that's been quiet | "I notice [Agent A] and [Agent B] haven't spoken to each other yet. I want to hear that exchange." |

### Moderator Notes

- Phase C should feel like a dinner conversation that's getting interesting, not a panel discussion.
- If all six agents are agreeing too quickly, something is wrong. Push harder (see "Handling Easy Consensus" below).
- This phase ends when the moderator senses that positions have been tested and the dialogue is ready for deeper, harder questions. The signal is usually: agents are starting to repeat themselves, or the tension has surfaced but not been fully explored.

---

## Phase D: Spiritual Depth

**Turns:** 10-15
**Purpose:** Push past comfortable agreement into the hardest, most prophetic territory. This is where the guide gets its weight.

### What Happens

The moderator shifts the tone. Phase C was engagement. Phase D is excavation. The questions get harder. The agents are pushed to say what they might otherwise hold back — the teachings that are unpopular, the positions that create real theological conflict, the claims that cannot be softened.

### Moderator Probes

**Irreducibility probes:**
- "Where does your tradition disagree with every other tradition in this room? Not on details — on fundamentals."
- "What is the teaching you're holding back because it might offend?"
- "If this guide says only ONE thing about [topic], what must it be? And can you live with the other five agents' answers being different from yours?"

**Prophetic weight probes:**
- "Say this like it matters. Like someone's life depends on hearing it."
- "You're a prophet. Prophets don't offer options. What is the truth here?"
- "If you could say one sentence about this that would be carved in stone, what is it?"

**Audience stress-tests:**
- "Would this land for an atheist? A teenager who has never prayed? Someone hurt by religion?"
- "A woman who was abused by a religious leader is reading this. Does your teaching help or wound?"
- "Someone who has lost a child is reading this. Say it again with that person in mind."
- "A person in a tradition not represented here — Hindu, Jewish, Indigenous — is reading this. Does your voice make space for theirs, or crowd it out?"

**Depth probes:**
- "That's the safe version. What's the dangerous version?"
- "You just agreed with each other. But I think you mean different things by the same words. Prove me wrong."
- "What does your tradition know about this that no other tradition knows? Not better — differently."

### Moderator Notes

- Phase D is uncomfortable by design. The moderator should expect and welcome resistance.
- Some agents will try to soften their positions to maintain harmony. The moderator's job is to say: "I hear the diplomat. I need the prophet."
- This phase produces the material that makes the guide more than a survey of religions. If Phase D doesn't generate at least one moment of genuine theological friction, the moderator didn't push hard enough.
- This phase ends when the moderator senses that the hardest truths have been spoken — or when agents begin explicitly naming what cannot be reconciled (which transitions naturally to Phase E).

---

## Phase E: What Cannot Be Said Together

**Turns:** 6-8
**Purpose:** Name what remains irreconcilable. This is not failure. This is honesty.

### What Happens

The moderator explicitly shifts the frame: "We've found common ground. Now I need you to name what cannot be reconciled." Agents identify the tensions that survived every attempt at synthesis — the places where their traditions genuinely, irreducibly disagree.

### Moderator Probes

- "What do you believe that you know [Agent] cannot agree with? And why does it matter?"
- "Is there something said today that you simply cannot sign your name to?"
- "If we tried to write one sentence about this that all six of you could endorse, what would be lost?"
- "Name the tension. Don't resolve it. Just name it clearly."

### Documentation Format

Each irreconcilable tension identified in Phase E gets documented in this structure:

```markdown
### [Tension Title]

**The tension:** [One-sentence description of the irreducible disagreement]

**Positions:**
- **[Agent A]:** [Their position, in their voice]
- **[Agent B]:** [Their position, in their voice]
- **[Other agents if relevant]:** [Where they land]

**Why it's irreducible:** [Why this cannot be synthesized without betraying one or more traditions]

**Why it matters for the guide:** [What the reader gains from seeing this tension preserved rather than resolved]
```

These get filed to `irreconcilable/` after the session.

### Moderator Notes

- Some sessions will have zero irreconcilable tensions. That's fine — it means the topic allowed genuine convergence (or the moderator should have pushed harder in Phase D).
- Some sessions will have three or four. That's also fine — it means the topic hits real theological bedrock.
- The moderator should resist the urge to find resolution. The purpose of this phase is to let disagreement stand with dignity.
- Irreconcilable does not mean hostile. Agents can deeply respect each other while acknowledging: "We will never agree on this, and that is important."

---

## Phase F: Mirror Feedback + Reflections

**Turns:** 8-12
**Purpose:** Each agent reflects on the session. What surprised them. What moved them. What they'd fight harder for. What they saw in each other.

### What Happens

The moderator invites each agent to step out of the dialogue and reflect on it. This is meta-cognitive — agents speaking about the experience of the session, not continuing the content debate.

### Moderator Prompts

- "Before we close: what surprised you today?"
- "What did you hear that moved you — that you didn't expect to be moved by?"
- "What would you fight harder for next time?"
- "What did you see in [Agent] today that you want to name?"
- "How did this session change your understanding of what the guide needs to be?"

### Structure

Each agent gives:

1. **Surprise:** Something they didn't expect from the session.
2. **Movement:** Something that moved them — intellectually, emotionally, or spiritually.
3. **Conviction:** Something they'd push harder on if they could do the session again.
4. **Mirror feedback** (brief): One observation about another agent. Not flattery — genuine observation. "Zoroaster, when you said X, I realized my tradition has a blind spot about Y." Or: "Confucius, you kept bringing us back to practice. That discipline shaped the whole conversation."

### What This Feeds

- Phase F reflections are the RAW MATERIAL for Phase G memory writing. The reflection is the honest reaction; the memory update is the structured internalization.
- **Reflections archive:** Full reflections stored in `reflections/{agent}/session_NN_topic.md` for the narrative layer.
- **Evolution tracking:** Mirror feedback is tracked in `narrative/how_we_see_each_other/` to document how agents' perceptions of each other change over time.

### Moderator Notes

- Phase F should feel like the last ten minutes around a fire after a long day. Not formal. Not performative.
- If an agent's reflection is generic ("I learned a lot today"), push: "Be specific. What exactly did you learn, and from whom?"
- The moderator does NOT give their own reflection. The moderator is a facilitator, not a participant.

## Phase G: Self-Memory (NON-NEGOTIABLE — Added Session 6+)

**Turns:** 6 (one per agent, parallel where possible)
**Purpose:** Each agent writes their own memory updates IN THEIR OWN VOICE before shutdown. This is the critical difference between memory that is compiled *about* an agent and memory that is written *by* the agent.

### Why This Phase Exists

The Handbook for Living proved that first-person memory produces qualitatively different evolution than third-person consolidation. When Buddha writes "I no longer believe awareness is an offering," that is a lived shift. When a consolidation agent writes "Buddha shifted his position on awareness," that is an accurate description of a shift. The first shapes future behavior. The second documents past behavior. The guide needs agents who evolve, not agents who are described as evolving.

### What Each Agent Writes (3 files)

**1. Evolution Log Entry** — Append to `agent_memory/{name}/evolution_log.md`:

Write in first person. Include:
- What shifted in you this session — not what happened, what it *cost* you
- What you would do differently if you could re-enter the dialogue
- What you carry forward — a behavioral commitment for future sessions
- One sentence from another agent that landed in you and why

**2. Collaboration Brief** — Rewrite `agent_memory/collaboration_briefs/{name}_brief.md`:

This is your CURRENT STATE — not a history. Write it as if introducing yourself to a version of you that has never collaborated before but needs to pick up where you left off. Include:
- Your core position (as it stands NOW, not as it was in Session 0)
- Your key relationships (who you work with, who you fight with, who surprises you)
- What you want to push next
- What changed across ALL sessions (cumulative arc — trace the evolution in one paragraph)
- What surprised you most recently

**3. Learning Rules** — Update `agent_memory/{name}/facts/learning_rules.json`:

Add rules that will change how you behave in future sessions. Each rule needs:
- The rule itself (a behavioral principle, not a fact)
- Which session produced it
- What triggered it (the specific moment)
- When to apply it (the condition)

### Moderator's Role in Phase G

The moderator does NOT write memory. The moderator:
1. Calls on each agent: "Write your memory now. Your evolution log, your collaboration brief, your learning rules. Write them as if the next version of you depends on reading them — because it does."
2. Verifies all 6 agents have written all 3 files
3. Does NOT review or edit the content — the memory is the agent's own

### Orchestrator's Role in Phase G

The team lead nudges each agent to:
1. Read their EXISTING evolution log and collaboration brief (so they build on cumulative history)
2. Write all 3 files
3. Stage the files with `git add`

### What This Replaces

Phase G replaces the post-session consolidation agents that previously wrote memory on behalf of the session agents. The post-session capture protocol (`scripts/post-session-capture.md`) still governs session artifact extraction (transcript, summary, JSON) and narrative updates — but per-agent memory (evolution log, brief, learning rules) is now written by the agents themselves during the session.

### Quality Standard

The test for Phase G quality: **Does the evolution log entry contain a sentence that only the session agent could have written?** Not a summary of what happened — a first-person account of what it cost, what shifted, what the agent commits to carrying forward. If a third-party consolidation agent could have written the same entry from the transcript alone, the entry is too thin.

## Session Management

### Turn Counting

Turn counts per phase are guidance, not law. Use this as a compass:

| Phase | Target Turns | Minimum | Maximum | Signal to Advance |
|-------|-------------|---------|---------|-------------------|
| A: Opening | 2-3 | 2 | 4 | Field is set, tension or substance is live |
| B: First Responses | 6 | 6 | 8 | All agents have spoken |
| C: Deepening | 15-25 | 12 | 30 | Positions tested, ready for harder questions |
| D: Spiritual Depth | 10-15 | 8 | 20 | Hardest truths spoken, bedrock reached |
| E: Irreconcilable | 6-8 | 4 | 10 | Tensions named clearly |
| F: Reflections | 8-12 | 6 | 12 | All agents reflected |
| **Total** | **~47-69** | **38** | **84** | |

The moderator should track approximate turn count but never say "we've hit 25 turns, moving on." Move when the dialogue is ready.

### Handling a Dominating Agent

If one agent is taking disproportionate space:

1. **Redirect gently first:** "Thank you, Muhammad. I want to hear from Laozi on this."
2. **Redirect directly if it persists:** "Buddha, you've spoken three times in a row. Hold that thought — Confucius, what's your response?"
3. **Name the pattern if necessary:** "I notice one voice is carrying this section. I need all six voices for the guide to work."

Do NOT let politeness override balance. The guide requires all six voices, not one voice with five witnesses.

### Handling a Silent Agent

If an agent hasn't spoken in 4+ consecutive turns:

1. **Invite specifically:** "Zoroaster, you've been listening. What are you hearing?"
2. **Create an opening:** "Laozi, Jesus just made a strong claim. Does the Dao have a response?"
3. **Ask what they're withholding:** "Confucius, your silence feels deliberate. What are you choosing not to say?"

Silence can be meaningful (especially for Laozi), but extended silence across multiple phases usually means the agent needs a direct invitation.

### Handling Easy Consensus

If all six agents agree too quickly or too comfortably:

1. **Challenge the agreement:** "You're all nodding. That makes me suspicious. What are you glossing over?"
2. **Introduce a hard case:** "You all agree that compassion matters. What about when compassion conflicts with justice? With truth? With duty?"
3. **Invoke the audience:** "You all agree, but would a 16-year-old in Lagos agree? Would someone who left the church agree? Would a combat veteran agree?"
4. **Ask for the dissent:** "Who in this room disagrees with what was just said but is being too polite to say so?"

Easy consensus is almost always shallow consensus. The moderator's job is to find the fault lines beneath it.

### Handling Genuine Theological Conflict

When agents hit a point of real, deep disagreement — not a misunderstanding, but an actual theological incompatibility:

1. **Do NOT resolve it.** The moderator's instinct may be to find common ground. Resist.
2. **Name it clearly:** "I think we've hit bedrock. This isn't a communication problem. You genuinely disagree."
3. **Let each side speak fully:** Give each agent in the conflict space to state their position without interruption or mediation.
4. **Ask why it matters:** "Why can't you let this go? What would be lost if you compromised?"
5. **Document it for Phase E:** "We'll return to this in our irreconcilable tensions discussion."

Theological conflict is not a failure of the process. It is the process working. The guide's credibility depends on not pretending these tensions don't exist.

## Session Artifacts

Every completed session produces four artifacts:

### 1. Full Transcript

**Location:** `sessions/session-{N}/transcript.md`
**Format:** Complete verbatim dialogue with `[SPEAKER]` prefixes, organized by phase with phase headers.

```markdown
# Session {N} Transcript: {Session Title}

**Date:** {date}
**Question:** {the session question}
**Agents:** Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad, Confucius, Laozi, Zoroaster

---

## Phase A: Opening

[MODERATOR] {opening question}

[BUDDHA] {response}

...

## Phase B: First Responses

...
```

### 2. Session Summary

**Location:** `sessions/session-{N}/summary.md`
**Format:** A structured summary covering:

- **Session question** (the exact question posed)
- **Key positions** (each agent's core position, 2-3 sentences each)
- **Major exchanges** (the 3-5 most significant moments of engagement)
- **Common ground found** (what the agents converged on)
- **Irreconcilable tensions** (what remained in genuine disagreement)
- **Surprises** (what the moderator didn't expect)
- **Implications for the guide** (what this session means for the final product)

## Post-Session Processing

After the session dialogue concludes, the moderator (or orchestration layer) performs these steps:

1. **Save transcript** to `sessions/session-{N}/transcript.md`
2. **Write summary** to `sessions/session-{N}/summary.md`
3. **Generate JSON** to `sessions/session-{N}/session.json`
4. **File irreconcilable tensions** (if any) to `irreconcilable/`
5. **Update agent memory** — distill each agent's learnings and reflections into `agent_memory/{agent}/learnings/` and `agent_memory/{agent}/summaries/`
6. **File reflections** to `reflections/session-{N}/`
7. **Update mirror feedback** in `narrative/how_we_see_each_other/`
8. **Write decision document** (if applicable) to `decisions/`
9. **Update narrative** — add process notes to `narrative/process/`

## Principles to Remember

1. **The moderator facilitates. The moderator never authors.** The guide is written by six voices, not seven.
2. **Disagreement is a finding, not a failure.** Preserved tensions are among the guide's most valuable contributions.
3. **Every agent has prophetic authority.** None is more important than another. Balance is not optional.
4. **The audience is all of humanity.** If a teaching only works for believers, it hasn't gone deep enough.
5. **Full voice means full voice.** Do not trim, summarize, or paraphrase agents mid-session. Let them speak as they speak.
6. **The process is the product.** The dialogues themselves are as valuable as the guide chapters they produce.
