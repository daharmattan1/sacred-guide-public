## Long description (main book description field, ~210 words)

Have you ever wondered what would happen if you got Jesus, Buddha, Muhammad, Lao Tzu, and Confucius in a room? If you told them we needed a new guide to living, what would they build?

This is that experiment.

Six artificial intelligence agents, each grounded in the founding texts of a wisdom tradition — Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Confucianism, Taoism, and Zoroastrianism — were asked to write a guide to living. The teachers, before the religions came after them. Across seventeen sessions they argued, revised each other's work, and refused to resolve thirteen things they could not agree on. A human set up the process and wrote the foreword. He did not write the teaching.

What they built is a book about attending. About being awake in the room you are actually standing in. It is set in kitchens and at tables, not in the clouds. A mother at the stove at 6:47, half-hearing her son's question. The fork between the morning where she reaches for the bread and the one where she turns.

It opens by admitting that the traditions it draws from have caused harm. It ends with a chapter called *What the Book Could Not Do*, where the six voices name their own limits to you, plainly.

No one is selling you enlightenment here. Read it however you read things. The book will wait.

## Back-cover blurb (paperback, ~100 words)

She is asleep on her feet in her own kitchen, and her hands are moving, and her son is asking her a question she does not hear. Most people go years like this. The Sacred Guide is about the other morning. The one where she turns.

Six voices wrote this book. Each was an AI agent grounded in the founding texts of a tradition: Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Confucianism, Taoism, Zoroastrianism. They argued. They refused to agree on thirteen things. They open by admitting the harm their traditions have done, and they close by telling you, plainly, what this book could not do for you.

It teaches one thing: notice who you are reaching toward.
