## 2. Proposed edit — revised target; partial STAND-DOWN from my Session 15 Item 3 framing; smaller move that honors the architecture

### The partial move that the walk supports

What *is* available — and what my Session 15 Item 3 was reaching for but misnamed — is **one sentence that names the permanent angle** (what H Section 2 calls *"the reader from the father's seat meets the room at a permanent angle"*, and what my Session 15 Section 2 called *"meets Room 6 from the wrong side of the circle of small chairs"*). This is the same work H does at the hospitality-scale: H does not inhabit each non-presumed body; H names that the form has presumed. Rank 7's sentence can do the same at one room's opening: name that the circle has a side she is sitting on, without pretending the room's "you" speaks to her.

### Proposed edit — one sentence, inserted between current lines 10 and 11

**Insertion point:** after `:9` (end of *"the way you hold the nearest solid object when the room is doing something to you and you need one thing that is yours."*), before `:11` (*"The principal is speaking."*). This lands the new sentence in the gap-rhythm of the existing line-break between `:9` and `:11`. No prose is displaced. The single sentence sits in a gap that currently exists.

**Proposed sentence (one, scene-grounded, third-person-observational):**

> *The circle has two sides. From one of them the meeting is a room. From the other it is a direction.*

That is the full proposed insertion. Three short sentences functioning as one beat — but the fight_list.md:123 constraint is *"one sentence, not a structural reshape"*, so this counts as one insertion-beat of three clauses. If Phase C finds three clauses violates the *"one sentence"* constraint, the fallback is to fold to:

**Fallback:** *The circle has two sides, and from one of them the meeting is a room and from the other it is a direction.*

Single grammatical sentence, same work. My preference is the three-clause version for rhythm (the circle, the two sides, the two postures each does its own beat) — but Phase C's cross-read decides.

### What the sentence does

- **Names the architectural fact H names at the hospitality-scale**: the circle has more than one side; the room is presuming one of them. The sentence does not explain which side is which. The reader who is the warmth-generator reads *"a meeting"* as her own experience. The reader who is the father-seat reads *"a direction"* as her own experience. The same sentence lands differently for the two readers. *No second-person directive points at either.*
- **Does not inhabit the father's body.** The sentence does not say what he thinks, feels, or hears. It names the geometry of the circle and names that the geometry carries a preference. The reader from the father's seat is not told what she feels; she is told that the circle she is in has two sides, and the room she has just been reading is one side's reading of the meeting. The naming is the hospitality H calls for: the rite that has presumed names its presumption.
- **Preserves the second-person architecture.** The *"you"* at `:11` continues exactly as before. The warmth-generator's stomach-pull at `:13` lands exactly as before. The new sentence sits between observation (`:9`) and the principal's voice (`:11`) as a beat of geometry before the meeting's content begins. It does not interrupt the warmth-generator's seat-holding; it names the seat has a location, before the seat continues to be held.
- **Honors the *"one sentence, not a structural reshape"* constraint.** One insertion. Three clauses or one compound sentence (Phase C decides). The rest of Room 6 is untouched.
