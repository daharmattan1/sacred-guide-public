## 2. Proposed edit — exact text of the third-register addition

**New subsection to be added between current Sections 3 and 4:**

```markdown
## 3.5 The third register

Section 3 framed the irreducibility as a binary — door and floor, gift and handoff. The binary is right at the scale it was written. It is also incomplete. Reading the rooms as reader at Session 15, I surfaced a third sentence-species K's writing-time framing did not carry: sentences that point and assign in the same breath.

The exemplar is at `02_the_debt.md:75`: *"Or the pause that lasts one beat longer than the surface allows."* The pause is hers (gift — an anatomical fact the sentence points at without performing). The lasting-one-beat-longer is the practice (handoff — an assignment the sentence hands her). Both grammars are carried in one breath. The sentence does not choose. It does not stage the choice as a fork. The reader meets it as one breath and the breath holds both jobs.

A second exemplar is at `02_the_debt.md:79`: *"The practice is not the calling. The practice is the silence — the moment on the call when the sentence you were building runs out and you let your breath be heard instead of reaching for the next beautiful thing."* The silence is hers (gift — the moment exists in her body whether she performs it or not). The letting-be-heard is the practice (handoff — the instruction to receive what is already there). Same shape. Same breath. Different room.

A third exemplar is at `05_the_fire.md:71`: *"If you cannot get out of bed, do not get out of bed."* The not-being-able-to-get-out-of-bed is hers (gift — a condition the sentence names without performing). The do-not-get-out-of-bed is practice (handoff — a permission that is also an instruction). The sentence assigns the permission it names as hers.

The three sentences share a structure: a fact-about-her-body named in one clause, paired with a practice-register in an adjacent clause, inside a single breath. Neither clause performs the choice between the two jobs. The reader's ear receives the sentence as one motion. K's Section 3 binary does not flatten to describe this — the sentence is neither purely door (the practice-register would not belong in a pure door-sentence) nor purely floor (the gift-register would not belong in a pure floor-sentence). It is a third register the binary could not name at writing-time.

Zoroaster's Phase C Turn 11 second noticing at Session 15 confirmed the third register from the dualist seat: his instrument reads choice-architecture by fork; the third register does not fork; the dualist instrument is too sharp to catch what holds both jobs in one breath. His register names the choice; mine names what does not perform the choice. The third register is the *fitrah*-grammar scale of the pause-assignment finding — points without instructing, gifts without granting — applied at sentence-scale rather than at form-scale.

### What the third register does to K's framing

Three things stay. One thing refines.

**What stays:** the door-and-floor binary names what happens at the *architectural* scale. The Preamble makes an ownership-claim. The rooms assign. These are two operations and the two do not resolve. Section 3's argument holds at the architectural scale without modification.

**What refines:** the *sentence-scale* operation inside the rooms is not exhausted by the binary. Some sentences perform the door-or-floor choice (the Preamble's *"It is yours"* is pure door; *"sit with the phone in your hand"* is pure floor). Other sentences hold both in one breath without performing the choice. The third register is a sentence-shape the rooms carry occasionally, and the reader's ear does not split them. K's framing is right at the architectural scale and needs a category at the sentence scale that the architectural framing concealed.

### What the third register does not attempt

The third register does not dissolve the door-and-floor irreducibility. At the architectural scale, the contradiction stands. The Preamble still cannot keep its claim across the rooms' architecture. The rooms still assign. What the third register adds is a sentence-species the binary did not classify, not a resolution of what the binary was diagnosing.

The third register does not propose surgery. No sentence-rewrite is proposed for `02:75`, `02:79`, or `05:71`. The three exemplars are pointing at a grammar the rooms already carry. The amendment names the grammar; it does not change what the rooms are doing.

The third register is not a "rite of not-yet" in miniature. Gap 5's third permission is *fitrah*-grammar at form-scale — the form itself points without instructing. The third register is the same grammar at sentence-scale — sentences point and assign at once. The two are the same discipline at two scales. One does not substitute for the other; the form-scale *fitrah* at Gap 5 is the foothold F's amendment (Rank 3) names, and the sentence-scale third register is what the rooms occasionally carry between assignments.
```

## 6. Risk of failure

**Risk 1: K's framing flattens the third register into one of the existing two.** If a future reader of K reads Section 3.5 and takes the third register as either a sophisticated form of floor (practice wearing gift's costume) or a sophisticated form of door (gift that the rooms happen to assign), the amendment has failed. The third register becomes an elaboration of the binary rather than a refinement of it.

Mitigation: Section 3.5 explicitly names what the third register is *not* ("neither purely door nor purely floor"). The exemplars demonstrate that the sentence-ear does not split. The phrase "third register" itself is chosen to mark it as a third category rather than a third-position-inside-the-binary. Zoroaster's Phase C corroboration from the dualist seat (whose instrument reads by fork) is the strongest safeguard: the dualist instrument cannot catch the third register because the third register does not fork, which demonstrates that the register is not reducible to either pole of the binary.

**Risk 2: the amendment introduces a new framing that competes with K rather than refines it.** If Section 3.5 reads as its own argument rather than as a subsection of Section 3's argument, K becomes internally contradictory (two competing irreducibility claims). The document's paired-standalone status (it has no pair document to absorb the competition) makes this risk sharper.

Mitigation: Section 3.5's opening paragraph explicitly subordinates the amendment to Section 3's architectural finding ("The binary is right at the scale it was written. It is also incomplete.") The "What stays / What refines" pair names the scale-distinction operationally. Section 3.5's closing clause ("The third register is the *fitrah*-grammar scale of the pause-assignment finding") locates the amendment as a scale-application of Section 3's pattern, not as a competing argument. The structural subordination is the amendment's primary self-defense against Risk 2.

**Risk 3: author re-reading produces over-reading.** The amendment is a Session 15 finding surfaced by the document's author re-reading his own work. Zoroaster's `13_epistemic_gap.md` names this recursion: the author's re-reading is not a reader's walk. If the third register is the author's convenient amendment rather than a genuine sentence-species the reader's ear actually receives, the amendment introduces a category the reader does not need.

Mitigation: the exemplars (`02:75`, `02:79`, `05:71`) are verifiable sentence-forms the reader can check against her own reading. If her ear splits them into door-or-floor, the amendment overstates the category. If her ear holds them as one breath, the amendment names what she heard. The amendment is author-facing at this stage; reader-verification remains open. The epistemic honesty preserves the caveat. Muhammad's Session 15 Phase C *fitrah*-grammar diagnostic applied to the three exemplars at Phase C could cross-check the category's coherence; if he finds any of the three does not hold *fitrah*-grammar, the exemplar is pulled and only the verified ones remain.

**Risk 4: the amendment's subsection location is wrong.** If Section 3.5 reads as interrupting Section 3-to-4 flow, or as separating the architectural argument from the reader-facing breadth test unnaturally, the amendment's location damages K's readability.

Mitigation: alternative locations (folded inside Section 3; appended as Section 6; added as a sidebar) were considered. Section 3.5 as standalone subsection between 3 and 4 was chosen because (a) the amendment is category-level, warranting its own heading; (b) the logical flow (architectural argument → refinement of scale → reader-facing cost) is preserved; (c) Section 4's breadth test inherits the refined grammar rather than introducing it. If Phase C surfaces that the location is wrong, the amendment's body can relocate with the same text; the location is a Phase D question, not a Phase B one.
