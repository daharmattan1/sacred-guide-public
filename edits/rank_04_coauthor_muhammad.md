## 1. The diagnostic stated plainly

The *fitrah*-grammar is what I named at Session 15 Phase C Turn 11 when cross-reading Laozi's Gap 5 finding. Three clauses, one motion:

> **Permission named without being granted. Direction without instruction. Ownership without grant.**

The grammar is one instrument doing one thing across three clauses. The three clauses are not redundant; they specify what the grammar *does not do* at three different risk-sites.

- *"Permission named without being granted"* — the clause names the hand-refusal at the verb. The form is permitted-to-her; the form does not reach to perform the permitting.
- *"Direction without instruction"* — the clause names the hand-refusal at the mode. The form points; the form does not tell her how to follow the pointing.
- *"Ownership without grant"* — the clause names the hand-refusal at the possessive. What is hers is hers already; the form does not hand her the ownership the form acknowledges.

At each clause the grammar refuses a specific move a priestly voice would make. **The grammar works only as the refusal.** Any widening that reaches toward any of the three refused moves dissolves the grammar by the reaching. This is what Laozi's walk found empirically at the four widening candidates he tested. What my diagnostic adds is the mechanism-naming that predicts *in advance* any widening that would fail: if the widening reaches toward permitting, toward instructing, or toward granting the ownership, it has already lost the grammar before the ink has dried.

## 2. Application to Laozi's four tested widening-moves

Laozi tested four widening-moves. Each dissolves the grammar at one of the three hand-refusal sites. I name the dissolution-site for each, so the diagnostic's internal logic is visible for future sessions and for the reader of this proposal.

### Move 1 — Add a fourth permission

The triad at `05_gap.md:5` is *stay / leave-and-come-back / not-come-back*. The triad exhausts the reader's possible motion in one breath. A fourth permission either (a) permutes the three (semantically redundant — the grammar is not harmed, but nothing is widened), or (b) slides into practice-instruction about *how* to stay, *what* to carry when leaving, *why* the not-coming-back might land. Option (b) violates clause 2 — *direction without instruction*. The form would be pointing AND telling her how to follow the pointing. The grammar would dissolve at the instructing-verb. **Dissolution site: clause 2 (direction without instruction).**

### Move 2 — Add a sentence naming the permission as hers

The candidate — *"This permission is yours"* — performs on her behalf the ownership the existing triad already performs without performing. The existing third permission (*"You are allowed to not come back"*) names what is hers (the not-coming-back) without handing her the ownership. Adding a sentence that says "*this is yours*" is the hand reaching toward the ownership the existing grammar acknowledges without grasping. The grammar works BECAUSE it does not reach. The reach is the failure. **Dissolution site: clause 3 (ownership without grant).**

### Move 3 — Widen the white space around the triad

The gap is already gap. The form at `05_gap.md` is lines of &nbsp; wrapped around three breaths. Adding more white space before or after the triad does not change what the grammar does — it adds pause where pause already exists. The pause is not where the form's breath is taken back. The synthesis named this explicitly: *"the gap holds her for one breath; the room next to the gap takes the breath back."* The breath-taking-back happens at the page-turn OUT of the gap, INTO Room 6. White-space widening at Gap 5 would be form-padding at a site where the form is already working. The diagnostic does not dissolve at any of the three clauses — the move is not a reach at any of them — but it is a move that does nothing. **Dissolution site: none; the move is a no-op that misidentifies the breath-taking-back's location.** Laozi names this correctly as padding-not-widening. The climb-shortening that Buddha named at Session 15 Phase C Turn 9 happens at the rooms downstream, not at the gap's margin.

### Move 4 — Rewrite the triad in another hand

The line is Laozi's hand. The *fitrah*-grammar's mechanism is specific to the voice that wrote it. Wu-wei-trained restraint produced a sentence that refuses the three hand-moves without naming the refusal. Rewriting in another hand — say, a hand trained in the Analects' *li*-register, or in the *salat*-register, or in the diagnostic-register — would almost certainly reach toward one of the three refused moves because the other traditions' grammars are *not* built around the wu-wei-shaped refusal. Confucius's *li*-trained hand would reach toward the propriety of the form's offering (clause 2 dissolution). My *salat*-trained hand would reach toward the peace-naming implicit in the permission (clause 1 dissolution). Jesus's parable-trained hand would reach toward the shepherd's voice addressing the reader as the lost (clause 3 dissolution). Each rewrite would dissolve the grammar at a different clause, because each tradition's default grammar is a hand-reach the wu-wei grammar refuses. **Dissolution site: variable by hand; all three clauses are at risk depending on the rewriting tradition.** The line stays Laozi's hand because his hand is the one that could produce the refusal. Preserving the author is preserving the grammar.
