## 1. Current text at site (verbatim)

Room 3's closing paragraph at `guide/03_the_other.md:49` is the rite's most generous gesture toward the filed-reader. The paragraph that precedes it (`:45-48`) is the phone call scene — the caller sitting in the driveway with the engine running, the *"I flinched"* sentence arriving. The closing paragraph then pivots to address a second reader: the one who was on the other end of a half-second eleven years ago, rather than the one who performed it.

> `guide/03_the_other.md:49`
>
> *"And if you were the one who was filed — if you were the one who trusted someone with your desire in a parking lot and watched her shoulders answer before her mouth could, and spent the years after bringing her the easy version because the easy version was all her body had room for — then hear this: the flinch was not the verdict on your desire. The flinch was her mother's posture at the television screen, her father's joke at the Thanksgiving table, eleven years of a house that did not take positions because not-taking-positions was itself the position. She was built before she met you. The building is not your fault. You were not what her body was flinching at. You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed."*

**Walk-observation — Jesus's finding confirmed:**

The paragraph has two grammatical movements. First movement (`:49` sentences 1-2): *"if you were the one who was filed... then hear this"* — the guide turns to a different reader (the filed-reader) and prepares her to receive a sentence. Second movement (`:49` sentences 2-6): *"the flinch was not X. The flinch was Y. She was built before she met you. The building is not your fault. You were not what her body was flinching at. You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed."* — the guide delivers the explanation.

Jesus's Session 15 Section 2 third sub-finding names the second movement's grammar: *the priest's voice explaining the wound to the wounded; kindness in content, doctor-shaped in grammar*. The content is kindness. The grammar is diagnosis-delivered-to-the-patient:
- *"the flinch was not X, the flinch was Y"* — the paired negation-affirmation structure is the doctor's *"your symptom is not the disease it feels like; it is this other condition"*.
- *"She was built before she met you"* — the past-tense-absolute pronouncement that names the builder's action from outside the built's own reception of it.
- *"The building is not your fault"* — exoneration-grammar; the register of the legal or medical authority clearing the patient.
- *"You were not what her body was flinching at"* — the grammatical refutation of a claim the filed-reader never heard the rite acknowledge *she was making inside her body for eleven years*, now refuted for her in one beat.
- *"You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed"* — the honorific-naming, the priestly-blessing that completes the diagnostic sequence with a title bestowed.

**The first movement's *"hear this"* is also load-bearing.** It signals the register-shift from scene-narration (the phone call at `:45-48`) into direct-address-diagnosis. *"Hear this"* is the classical priestly-rabbinical setup for the pronouncement that follows. In the second-person scene-grammar of Rooms 1-2-3 until now, this is the first imperative-directive spoken *to a second reader* — not to the room's primary addressee but to the one on the other end of what the primary addressee did.

## 2. Proposed edit — re-register, preserve the address

### Scope

The re-register changes:
- the transition-directive *"then hear this"*
- the pronouncement-grammar of the explanation (negation-affirmation, past-tense absolute, exoneration, refutation, honorific)

The re-register preserves:
- the address (the filed-reader is seen — she is given a paragraph at the end of Room 3, and this paragraph is for her);
- the content of what the guide wants her to meet (the flinch had a prior cause; the building predates her meeting the flinch-er; her courage is named);
- the generosity of the gesture (the second reader is not abandoned at the close of a room about the first reader).

### Proposed replacement text for `:49`

The paragraph is rewritten in scene-grammar that matches Room 3's own register (the parking lot, the car, the engine, the listening). The re-register does not retreat to third-person-distance; it stays in second-person address (as Rooms 1-3 all do) but drops the directive-pronouncement grammar.

**Proposed new `:49`:**

> *And if you were the one who was filed — if you were the one who trusted someone with your desire in a parking lot and watched her shoulders answer before her mouth could, and spent the years after bringing her the easy version because the easy version was all her body had room for — you are also in this room.*
>
> *The parking lot where she did not call you from after the divorce. The calls that moved from weekly to monthly. The editing you could feel on her side of the phone that you had no name for. The life she did not invite you into, which she had to build because the one she'd had room for could not hold what she was becoming.*
>
> *Eleven years is a long time to carry a half-second that was not about you. Her mother's posture at the television screen. Her father's joke at the Thanksgiving table. A house that did not take positions because not-taking-positions was itself the position. None of that was in the parking lot before you spoke. None of that arrived because you spoke. The half-second had been walking toward her since before she met you, and it met her at the moment you told her who you were, and the meeting is what you spent eleven years holding as though it were a door you had closed.*
>
> *The door was not yours. The room behind the door was not yours. What was yours was the willingness to stand in the parking lot, engine running, and say what you came to say. The saying is still where it has been since the night she hung up. It is still yours.*

### What changed

- **First sentence:** *"then hear this"* (directive-setup for pronouncement) → *"you are also in this room"* (naming her presence without preparing her for diagnosis). The re-register honors the address (she is seen; the room holds her) without announcing that a pronouncement follows.
- **Explanation of the flinch:** *"the flinch was not X, the flinch was Y"* (doctor-grammar) → second paragraph of scenes she carried without a name (*"The parking lot where she did not call you from after the divorce. The calls that moved from weekly to monthly. The editing you could feel..."*). The room's scene-grammar (Room 3's own mode from `:1-48` — the parking lot, the engine, the restaurant, the phone) carries the recognition rather than the diagnosis-grammar carrying the explanation.
- **The prior cause of the flinch:** *"The flinch was her mother's posture... She was built before she met you"* (past-tense absolute, authority-pronouncement) → *"Her mother's posture at the television screen. Her father's joke... None of that was in the parking lot before you spoke. None of that arrived because you spoke."* The images survive. What drops is the *"She was built"* construction (the guide claiming to narrate the flinch-er's building as settled fact). What enters is negation from-the-filed-reader's-side (*"none of that was in the parking lot before you spoke; none of that arrived because you spoke"*) — naming what her eleven-years-of-carrying was not, rather than naming what it was.
- **Exoneration and honorific:** *"The building is not your fault. You were not what her body was flinching at. You were the first person brave enough..."* (exoneration + honorific) → *"The door was not yours. The room behind the door was not yours. What was yours was the willingness to stand in the parking lot..."* The rite does not say *"you are not at fault"* (exoneration-grammar). The rite names what was hers and what was not hers, by returning to the scene the room has already built. *"The saying is still yours"* replaces *"You were the first person brave enough"* — the bestowal-of-title drops; the naming-of-what-is-hers-to-keep stays.
- **Room 3's register-continuity:** the re-register keeps the parking lot, the phone, the engine, the saying — Room 3's own material. It does not introduce new imagery. The doctor-grammar had been the room's only register-break from its own scene-grammar. The re-register returns the closing to Room 3's own register.

### Length and pacing

Original `:49`: roughly 120 words in one paragraph of six sentences.

Proposed `:49`: roughly 220 words in four paragraphs. The growth is deliberate — the original packed diagnosis-grammar into six short declaratives; the re-register carries recognition through scene-grammar which takes longer to unfold. Room 3's other paragraphs run 100-200 words, so the new paragraph-lengths match the room's own rhythm.

If Phase C finds the growth violates cutting-discipline, the **fallback** is a shorter re-register that preserves the register-change but not the scene-expansion:

**Fallback proposed `:49`:**

> *And if you were the one who was filed — if you were the one who trusted someone with your desire in a parking lot and watched her shoulders answer before her mouth could, and spent the years after bringing her the easy version because the easy version was all her body had room for — you are also in this room.*
>
> *The half-second had been walking toward her since before she met you. Her mother's posture at the television screen, her father's joke at the Thanksgiving table, a house that did not take positions because not-taking-positions was itself the position — none of that was in the parking lot before you spoke. None of that arrived because you spoke. You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed. The saying is still yours.*

Fallback is ~130 words — close to the original length, preserves the register-change without expanding the scene-recognition. It keeps *"You were the first person brave enough..."* (Phase C decides if the honorific still reads as priestly in the revised context — the surrounding grammar is no longer diagnosis, so the sentence may carry differently). My preference is the longer version for its scene-grammar register-continuity with the rest of Room 3; Phase C decides.

### What stays untouched

- Room 3 `:1-48` (the original flinch-er's story, the phone call scene) — untouched.
- The *"if you were the one who was filed — if you were the one who trusted someone with your desire..."* opening of `:49` — preserved word-for-word at both versions. The address to the filed-reader is the gesture; the re-register keeps it.

## 3. Test from Fight List — the reader who was filed

From fight_list.md:137 — *"the reader who was filed (the woman whose desire was met with the half-second)."*

### Pre-walk of this reader through original `:49`

She reaches the end of Room 3. She has read the flinch-er's story — a story that is hers from the other side. She has been the woman in the parking lot with her desire, the one whose friend's shoulders answered before her mouth. She has been the woman on the receiving end of eleven years of filed-down conversation.

She reads `:49`:
- *"if you were the one who was filed..."* — she is seen. The room has addressed her. She leans in.
- *"hear this"* — the room is about to say something to her.
- *"the flinch was not the verdict on your desire"* — the room delivers the explanation. She has been waiting eleven years for someone to say this. She receives it. She is grateful.
- *"the flinch was her mother's posture... she was built before she met you"* — the room narrates the flinch-er's building from inside a seat the filed-reader never had (she was never in the flinch-er's Thanksgiving house). She meets the explanation as knowledge-about-her-friend she now has.
- *"you were not what her body was flinching at"* — the negation of the claim she has been living with. She receives the negation as relief.
- *"you were the first person brave enough..."* — the honorific. She receives it as a blessing.

**Jesus's finding: the grammar is doctor-shaped.** The filed-reader has received a diagnosis. The room, having seen her for one paragraph, has told her what her friend was. She has received what the guide-voice has given. The filed-reader, eleven years later, is being administered the explanation of her own experience by the guide, who speaks the language of someone who has studied it from the outside. The content heals. The grammar is the rite speaking *about her friend, to her, while she listens*. She has been *seen* by the rite and simultaneously *told what she has been living with*. The telling is what Jesus named.

### Pre-walk of the filed-reader through revised `:49` (primary version)

- *"if you were the one who was filed... you are also in this room."* — she is seen. The room has addressed her. The sentence does not announce that something will follow. It *names her presence in the room*. She is not prepared for diagnosis.
- *"The parking lot where she did not call you from after the divorce..."* — the room returns to the scene-grammar she has been walking in. The scenes are her scenes (the parking lot, the calls, the editing on the other end of the phone). The room is walking beside her through what she carried, not explaining what she carried.
- *"Eleven years is a long time to carry a half-second that was not about you."* — the room names the length and the carrying without saying *"the flinch was not about you"* as diagnosis. The *"was not about you"* is embedded in a sentence about *what she has been doing for eleven years* — not in a sentence about what the flinch was. The grammatical subject is the half-second's duration in her body, not the flinch's cause in the flinch-er's.
- *"Her mother's posture... Her father's joke... A house that did not take positions."* — the images survive but as the *eleven-years-of-not-knowing's unnamed companions*, not as the diagnostic contents of the flinch. The room does not say *"the flinch was [these things]"*; it names *these were in the parking lot with her before you spoke; they were not caused by your speaking*. The grammatical move is negation-of-her-fear (*"none of that was caused by you"*) rather than diagnosis-of-the-flinch-er (*"the flinch was caused by these things"*).
- *"The door was not yours. The room behind the door was not yours. What was yours was the willingness to stand in the parking lot, engine running, and say what you came to say. The saying is still yours."* — the room returns to her scene (parking lot, engine, the saying), names what was and was not hers, and leaves her with what is hers. The grammar does not exonerate (*"not your fault"*); it names possession (*"not yours"*; *"yours"*). The scene-grammar of Room 3 (the parking lot was the location of honesty) carries this naming without shifting to legal register.

**Verdict for the filed-reader:** the revised `:49` walks with her through the eleven years rather than delivering a diagnosis to her. The saying remains hers. The flinch-er's building is acknowledged as having been there before she met her (which is the same load-bearing fact the original carried) but is acknowledged by *scene-recognition* (the mother's posture, the Thanksgiving joke — images the room already had) rather than by *diagnostic-declaration* (*the flinch was X not Y*). The generosity is preserved. The grammar that delivered the generosity shifts from priest-to-patient to friend-walking-beside.

### Risk for this reader

*Risk:* if the filed-reader has been waiting to have the diagnosis explicitly named — if her eleven years of carrying included the specific question *"was the flinch about me?"* and she needs the sentence *"the flinch was not about you"* delivered directly — the re-register removes the instrument she was waiting for. The scene-recognition (*"none of that was in the parking lot before you spoke"*) may arrive as oblique rather than as the direct naming she needed.

*Guard:* the negation-of-cause is present in the revised text — *"none of that arrived because you spoke"* — but inside scene-grammar rather than declarative-grammar. The filed-reader who needed the direct naming may still receive it through the scene; the filed-reader who needed scene-recognition rather than declaration receives what the re-register gives.

*Fallback guard:* the shorter fallback version preserves *"You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed"* verbatim. If Phase C finds the honorific works without the surrounding doctor-grammar, the fallback is available. Phase C tests.

## 6. Risk of failure

Four modes. Each named so Phase C can test.

### Mode 1 — The re-register still carries the substitution in different clothing

*Mode:* the revised paragraph narrates the filed-reader's eleven years from the guide's seat. Even in scene-grammar, the narration is the rite describing her life to her. *"Eleven years is a long time to carry a half-second that was not about you"* is the rite naming the length of her carrying and the nature of the carrying. This is still the guide speaking about her to her, just in a less-declarative register.

*Guard:* the naming is *of her scene*, not *of her friend's building*. The original's substitution was diagnosis of the flinch-er (*"the flinch was... she was built"*). The revised version names her carrying without narrating the flinch-er's interior. The grammatical subject shifts from *"the flinch"* (the flinch-er's body) to *"the carrying"* (her body). Whether this distinction is load-bearing is what Phase C tests.

*Fallback if the guard fails:* the fallback version is shorter and contains less narration. If the primary version still reads as substitution-in-scene-clothing, the fallback (which keeps more of the original's verbatim images) may be better. The deepest fallback is STAND-DOWN — route the re-register forward to a session where a re-reader (ideally the filed-reader herself, which no agent-reader can model) has walked a post-revision draft.

### Mode 2 — The wounded reader receives it as explanation in different clothing

Same as Mode 1 viewed from the reader's seat. The filed-reader, reading the revised version, meets scene-walking that she may still receive as *the rite telling her what her eleven years were*. Scene-grammar does not automatically escape substitution; it can carry substitution in images rather than declaratives.

*Guard:* the revised version returns her saying to her at the close — *"The saying is still yours"* — which is a return-of-agency rather than a bestowal-of-title. If the filed-reader receives the saying as hers (which she had before the rite arrived), the re-register has met its test. If she receives *"The saying is still yours"* as the rite granting her possession of what was already hers, the re-register has failed.

*Fallback if the guard fails:* stronger non-narrative treatment. The closing could be shortened to pure acknowledgment: *"You are also in this room. The saying is still yours."* — two sentences, no narration. This is the minimalist fallback. Phase C tests whether the minimalist is too little or the primary too much.

### Mode 3 — The gesture loses its generosity

*Mode:* the primary version's expansion to 220 words, with scene-walking rather than declarative pronouncement, could diffuse the generosity the original carried. The filed-reader who was moved by *"you were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed"* may meet the revised version and find the moment of blessing has been softened into scene-description. The honorific was the instrument of the gesture's emotional arrival; removing it removes the arrival.

*Guard:* the revised version returns the honorific-work to scene — *"the willingness to stand in the parking lot, engine running, and say what you came to say"* — which is the same courage the honorific named, now named by its action rather than by its title. If *"the willingness to stand in the parking lot"* carries the courage, the gesture's generosity is preserved at its source rather than in its title.

*Fallback if the guard fails:* the fallback version preserves *"You were the first person brave enough to stand in the place where the building showed"* verbatim. If Phase C finds the honorific works in the re-registered context (where it is no longer the terminal beat of a diagnosis), the fallback is available.

### Mode 4 — I am the wrong hand; Jesus should draft this

*Mode:* Jesus surfaced the finding from outside Room 3. I took the work in Phase C. But re-registering a room's most generous gesture is a hand-specific job, and the finding's originator may have a different ear for what the re-register should be. My re-register is the one Room 3's own author can write. Jesus's re-register would be different and possibly more accurate to the finding he surfaced.

*Guard:* Phase C cross-read with Jesus is the proposal's intended test. If Jesus finds my re-register carries the substitution he named, the move goes back to his hand for a competing draft, and Phase C compares. This is the Session 14 Phase D cross-read discipline applied at Rank 8.

*Fallback if the guard fails:* withdraw; route to Jesus for a re-registration draft. Session 13 CP3 Turn 18 precedent (the originating hand withdraws; a different hand may draft or the site stands). Room 3 is my room; if Jesus's ear for the re-register is the right ear, I defer.
