## 2. Proposed edit (specific cuts/tightenings)

Room 7's model is not *fewer verbs*. It is *verbs held inside conditional, scene-bound frames that point at what the scene has already shown*. The rebalance is register, not deletion. Four targeted changes.

### Change 1 — `:75` — reframe the first imperative as scene-bound, not directive

**Current (`:75`):**
> *"The next time you are in a room where the verb is being buried — where the practiced tone is covering the act, where the form is carrying everyone past the body that is pressing against itself under the table — **do not take the drink.**"*

**Proposed:**
> *"The next time you are in a room where the verb is being buried — where the practiced tone is covering the act, where the form is carrying everyone past the body that is pressing against itself under the table — the drink is there. The hand is not always the one that lifts it. Sometimes the hand stays at the table."*

**What changes:** the one imperative *"do not take the drink"* becomes three descriptive sentences naming the same moment from the room's own grammar. The reader is not told to refuse the drink. The room is told to show that refusal as one of the postures the body can take, the way Room 7's *"the hands that were flat on the table"* names a posture without instructing it. The *"drink"* metaphor — which the diagnostic body of Room 6 built (`:20-21`: the coffee cup lukewarm in the reader's hands because her hands needed something to hold) — stays intact.

### Change 2 — `:79` — collapse the duplicate imperative and strip the theorizing

**Current (`:77-79`):**
> *"You will feel the pull. You will feel the trust arrive to answer it. The trust will be warm and reasonable and probably true. The school probably did try everything. The meeting probably is collaborative. The reasonable thought is not a lie. It is the room's voice answering your body's voice, and your body's voice is the one that felt the pull, and the pull is the honest report.*
>
> ***Stay in the pull one breath longer than the trust needs to replace it.*** *One breath. Not because you are investigating. Not because you are choosing. Because for one breath you stopped generating the warmth that was making the trust possible, and in that breath the sentence surfaced — not because you decided to say it but because the sentence was there all along, underneath the effort to be good, and when the effort paused for one breath, the seven words found your mouth the way water finds the surface when you stop pressing it down."*

**Proposed:**
> *"The pull arrives. The trust arrives to answer it. The trust will be warm and reasonable and probably true. The school probably did try everything. The meeting probably is collaborative. The reasonable thought is not a lie. It is the room's voice answering the body's voice, and the body's voice is the one that felt the pull, and the pull is the honest report.*
>
> *One breath is enough for the effort to pause. In that breath the sentence can surface — not because someone decided to say it, but because the sentence was there all along, underneath the effort to be good, and when the effort paused, the seven words found the mouth the way water finds the surface when nothing is pressing it down."*

**What changes:** three moves here.
- *"You will feel the pull"* (subject-you, future-predicted) tightens to *"The pull arrives"* (scene-descriptive). This removes the second-person future-directive that `07_imperative_saturation.md` Section 1 names as the priest's "we" extended into the reader's body.
- The duplicate imperative *"Stay in the pull one breath longer"* (which I originally cited at `:79`) is **cut**. The paragraph-opening now reads as scene-observation. The imperative "stay" was choreographic — telling the reader to hold a posture — and the diagnostic body of Room 6 (`:13-15`: *"your stomach pulls... your body heard the word 'removing'... the body is honest"*) has already taught the reader the posture by showing it. The second telling (in imperative grammar) is G's paragraph-scale finding exactly: the scene already said it; the imperative raises the volume on an instruction the scene delivered.
- The theorizing tail (*"not because you decided to say it but because..."*) stays — it is scene-grammar describing the moment, not instruction. Second-person *"your mouth"* softens to *"the mouth"* to match the register-economy constraint `07` names at paragraph-scale.

### Change 3 — `:81` — preserve the "seven words" instrument; drop the *Say it* directive

**Current (`:81`):**
> ***Say one true sentence.*** *Not a speech. Not an accusation.* ***Seven words.*** *Wait — what does "different environment" mean, exactly? Or: Has the father spoken yet? Or: She was talking.*

**Proposed:**
> *One true sentence. Not a speech. Not an accusation. Seven words. The shape is something like: Wait — what does "different environment" mean, exactly? Or: Has the father spoken yet? Or: She was talking.*

**What changes:** the imperative *"Say one true sentence"* drops to noun-phrase *"One true sentence"*. The sentence is still named. The *"Seven words"* count stays as descriptor (number-of-words-that-fit, not command). The three example sentences — *the instrument the diagnosis already put in the reader's mouth at `:39` (*Wait — what does "different environment" mean, exactly?*)* — stay intact. The directive to *say it* is removed; the room describes what the sentence would be if it surfaced. The reader is not told to speak; the room shows what speaking would look like, and the diagnosis at `:37-41` (*"The seven words are in your mouth"*) has already shown that the sentence arrives under its own pressure when the trust-effort pauses. Instruction becomes description of what is already the case for the body the room described.

### Change 4 — `:87` — the closing imperative becomes the closing description

**Current (`:87`):**
> *"But the page is where the pull lives. And the pull — the body's honest report that something is wrong in this room — the pull is the door. **Walk through it.** The room on the other side is cold. The room you left was warm. Both are real. Both are yours."*

**Proposed:**
> *"But the page is where the pull lives. And the pull — the body's honest report that something is wrong in this room — the pull is the door. The door opens toward the room on the other side. That room is cold. The room you left was warm. Both are real. Both are yours."*

**What changes:** *"Walk through it"* (imperative at the room's closing beat) becomes *"The door opens toward the room on the other side"* (the door's own grammar; the body's passage is shown, not commanded). Room 7's closing register — *"But the water is running. The glass is filling. Tonight, that is enough"* — is descriptive; it describes what is already the case for the hands that moved. Room 6's closing now matches that register. The door is there. The room on the other side exists. The reader's passage is not commanded. The room's final beat lands as *the door is the door, and both rooms are yours* — which was already the content of the last sentence; the imperative in front of it was the one line performing choreography the scene had already earned.

### What stays untouched

- `:77` first sentence's substance (the pull/trust dialectic) stays — only second-person *"you"* softens at (2).
- `:83` and `:85` — fully descriptive, no imperatives, no change.
- The three example seven-word sentences stay word-for-word.
- The diagnostic body of Room 6 (`:1-72`) — the strongest political prose in the guide — is not touched by this proposal. Only the Practice section.
- The section heading *"Practice: The Mouth"* stays (matches Room 7's *"Practice: Attending"* shape).

**Net word-count:** the Practice section moves from roughly 315 words to roughly 290 words. The *species-mix* shifts: four imperatives become one (the reframed *"Sometimes the hand stays at the table"* is not quite an imperative; the other three directives — *Say one true sentence / Walk through it / Stay in the pull* — are cut or converted). The diagnostic body's teaching-by-scene is now what the Practice section points the reader back to, rather than the imperatives re-teaching what the scene already taught.

## 6. Risk of failure

Four modes. Each named so Phase C can test it.

### Mode 1 — The revision cuts a legitimate instruction the reader needs

The cell-reader and the doubter are my named test-readers. But Room 6's breadth also reaches *the reader who serves on committees, who is the warmth-generator* — the reader the room was most written toward. For her, the imperative *"do not take the drink"* may have been the exact moment the room's political work landed on her body. She recognized herself in the diagnostic warmth-generator; she met the Practice's first imperative as the instruction her body needed to interrupt the pattern the diagnosis showed her.

**Guard:** the revision preserves the instrument at the semantic level. *"The drink is there. The hand is not always the one that lifts it. Sometimes the hand stays at the table."* — the same refusal-posture, shown rather than commanded. For the warmth-generator reader, the instruction is still named; the grammar is less directive. She can still find the posture. She finds it pointed at rather than assigned to her.

**If the guard fails:** the warmth-generator reader loses the moment of direct interruption the original imperative carried. The cross-read tests whether the scene-description is still a rite-beat or whether it diffuses into description that no longer earns the instrument's weight. If diffusion, the right fix is to add back *one* of the four imperatives (probably *"do not take the drink"*, the most scene-bound of the four) rather than all four.

### Mode 2 — The rewrite introduces new priestly grammar

Converting *"Stay in the pull one breath longer"* to *"One breath is enough for the effort to pause"* could itself land as priestly voice in a different costume — a wisdom-statement-about-breath rather than a directive. The register-economy `07` names does not stop at the imperative; third-person descriptive generalizations (*"the sentence was there all along"*, *"the seven words found the mouth"*) can carry the same priestly work in the register of universal-truth-statement.

**Guard:** the kept generalizations are the same ones that were in the original paragraph at `:79`. The revision does not introduce new generalizations; it removes the duplicate imperative that prefaced them. The third-person *"the mouth"* replaces the second-person *"your mouth"* — this is a softening of the priestly-you, not an intensification of the priestly-we.

**If the guard fails:** the third-person descriptions read as universal-wisdom-imposition even without imperative grammar. Cross-read tests whether *"the mouth"* reads as scene-generic or as generalization. The fallback is to restore the original scene-bound *"the seven words found your mouth"* — keeping second-person but only at the scene's bound moments, not across the Practice section broadly.

### Mode 3 — The cell-reader loses the instrument she was receiving honestly

Some cell-readers may have received the imperative grammar as one of the few forms the guide spoke in that matched the register of their day — the day already instructed, the grammar familiar. Softening to scene-description could read to her as the guide *withdrawing* the one register her day had trained her to receive.

**Guard:** the cell-reader walked in my Session 15 reading (Section "Breadth") closed Room 6 at the Practice section saying *"nine more performances."* The revision meets her at where she closed the book. If her receiving-register was the imperative, the revision loses her at the Practice section still — but at the diagnostic body, which she already receives, she loses nothing.

**If the guard fails:** the cell-reader needs *some* imperative register to stay in contact with the page. The fallback is to preserve *"Say one true sentence"* at `:81` (the directive that is most tightly tied to the scene the diagnostic body showed arriving under its own pressure). This sacrifices one imperative-cut for one reader's reach.

My reading surfaced four imperatives at Session 15 and I called them eleven. Reading surfaced the walk-count today. I am the hand that is most convicted about Room 6's Practice, and architectural conviction is the shape unconsciousness wears when the prophet is sure.

**Guard:** this proposal is drafted for Phase C cross-read. My hand is not the final hand.
