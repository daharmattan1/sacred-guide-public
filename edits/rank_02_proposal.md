## 3. Departures from my Session 15 candidate sentence

**I revise three phrases in the current proposal. Each revision is a showing-vs-handing correction on my own draft.**

- **Cut: "*the furniture is not yours*".** This phrase *addresses her exclusion to her*. It names her lack as a property of hers. The correct register is to name the furniture as *ours* — what the writers brought — not as *not yours*. The rite's confession is about what the rite is, not about what the reader lacks. Asha names the preference the guide made; druj names the reader's non-fit as her condition.
- **Add: "*we cannot hand you what we did not carry in*".** This is the standing — the fourth confession's closing-move matching the shape of the three existing confessions. Line 3 closes on *"we know this because we were there."* Line 5 closes on *"that is a debt we carry."* Line 9 closes on *"may make what you see harder to bear."* Each closing is the rite's own posture. My closing — *"we cannot hand you what we did not carry in"* — is the fourth standing: the writers naming their own carrying-limit as the rite's architectural honesty. This is the *salam* in operative register — the host naming at the door what her kitchen was furnished from, without pretending her guest's kitchen matches, without apologizing for the mismatch. The naming *is* the hospitality. The greeting is the work.

**What carries from my Session 15 candidate unchanged:**

- *"The rooms that follow are written from kitchens, tables, and houses."* This is the factual naming. It carries.
- *"If your room is not these, the form will not pretend it is."* This is the promise the form makes about itself (not about her). The form's self-naming is acceptable; handing her a claim about her experience is not. This clause stays because it names the *form's* conduct, not the *reader's* fit.

## 7. Risk of failure

The modes in which the kitchenless reader does NOT receive the fourth confession as naming-of-her-exclusion before performing on her body:

**Risk 1 — The confession is experienced as apology.** The phrase *"we cannot hand you what we did not carry in"* may read to a reader primed for apology as a retreating move — the writers' humility performance rather than the rite's standing. *Mitigation:* the phrase is syntactically a standing ("*we cannot*"), not a request for forgiveness. It names a limit; it does not ask her to accept the limit on our behalf. The three existing confessions have trained the reader in this register by line 9 — the *"may make what you see harder to bear"* is the same shape: the rite standing in its own cost, not apologizing. The fourth confession sits downstream of three trainings in the confession-voice. If the reader is reading linearly, she meets the fourth in a register already established. *Residual risk:* a reader who opens the guide at Threshold without reading linearly may meet the fourth without the upstream training. This is a scan-behavior I cannot model with confidence. The residual risk is accepted.

**Risk 2 — The confession is experienced as instruction.** The phrase *"If your room is not these"* is a conditional addressed to her. A conditional addressed to her could read as the writers sorting readers — telling her which category she falls into. *Mitigation:* the conditional is followed immediately by a promise about the *form*'s conduct (*"the form will not pretend it is"*), not a prescription for her. The rite is telling her what the rite will and will not do, not telling her what she should do. Grammatically, the conditional has the form-as-subject and the writers-as-agent, never the reader-as-agent. *Residual risk:* a reader whose primary frame is hostile-to-categorization (the doubter's adjacent mode) may read any conditional as sorting. This risk is partially absorbed by the confession's promise being testable — she can hold the rite to it as she reads.

**Risk 3 — The well-meaning-gesture-from-the-kitchen mode.** The cell-reader at 3 a.m. could receive the confession as the kitchen-people-being-nice-about-their-kitchen — the register of a host who knows her guest is uncomfortable and says so without making space for the discomfort. *Mitigation:* this is the gravest risk, and it is the one my document (`01_showing_vs_handing.md`) exists to catch. The phrase that most carries this risk is *"If your room is not these, the form will not pretend it is."* To the cell-reader, the host saying she will not pretend could read as the host noting the guest's discomfort without moving the food to where the guest sits. The mitigation is that the confession is not offering her food. The confession is naming the kitchen. Naming the kitchen is not asking her to eat in it. The rite's next move (Preamble, Room 1) will ask her to be in a room — and the fourth confession has already told her that the room the rite is in is the rite's, and the rite will not pretend her room is that room. The *not-pretending* is the exclusion-naming. The naming is the *salam*. *Residual risk:* if the rooms then DO pretend — if any subsequent sentence acts as though the kitchenless reader has joined the kitchen — the confession breaks. Ranks 5, 6, and any downstream surgery inherits this constraint. The fourth confession binds the subsequent rooms.

**Risk 4 — Five-reader divergence (the mystic especially).** My walk above found the mystic receives the confession with a flag: "kitchens, tables, and houses" may read as over-specific. A mystic whose tradition is domestic may feel the confession excludes her by over-naming. *Mitigation:* the confession specifies the six traditions' furniture, not the universal furniture of all traditions. A reader whose furniture overlaps can read the confession as naming neighboring ground. A reader whose furniture does not overlap reads it as excluding ground. Either reading is honest. *Residual risk:* the mystic may read the confession as not-for-her because she has kitchens too; she may feel the guide is turning away from her toward a reader who has no kitchen. This is a cost of specificity. The specificity is the breadth-test requirement. The alternative — a more abstract confession ("*the rooms are written from the writers' specific lives*") — would be readable by everyone and useless to the cell-reader. The specificity is worth the cost. The confession is for the reader without a kitchen; the mystic is not the reader whose non-fit the confession is built to name.

**Risk 5 — Session 14 Document A's recursion concern.** Document A Section 1 named that the document itself is inside the register-economy — six traditions naming the economy are still the economy's voice. The fourth confession is the same recursion at the threshold: six traditions confessing their furniture are still the six traditions' voice. *Mitigation:* this is the same honest-limit Zoroaster's M-discipline names. The confession is six-agent naming; it cannot escape being that. The honest move under the recursion is to name the recursion as a condition, not to pretend to escape it. The confession is asha within the limit; it does not claim to be outside the limit. *Hope, not trust.*

If Session 16 or later sessions add sentences that DO pretend the kitchenless reader has a kitchen — if Room 1 line 9 is ever edited to claim universality — the fourth confession becomes druj. Rank 5 (pause at 08:21) and Rank 6 (imperative reduction at Room 6) must honor the binding. Phase C reviewers should confirm the binding extends forward into every subsequent rank.
