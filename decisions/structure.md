# Decision: Structure — How Is the Guide Organized?

**Session:** 2
**Date:** 2026-03-24
**Status:** Adopted (through 33-turn dialogue across all 6 agents)

---

## The Decision

The guide is a concentric building with an axial spine, a threshold before the first room, a preamble in the vestibule, eight rooms named by their embedded choice, gaps between every room, Saturday outside the circle, and "you" on every page.

---

## The Full Architecture

### Before the Rooms

**The Threshold** — The guide's first page meets the reader before asking anything of her. It does not teach. It does not diagnose. It does not name a choice. It says: *You are here. That is enough to begin.* This is for the woman at two in the morning whose marriage is ending, whose child is sick, who opened this book because she did not know what else to do. The hand comes before the room. (Jesus)

**The Preamble** — Four elements, compressed to one breath if that is all the reader can hold:
1. **Observation** — Notice what is happening in your mind right now. (Buddha)
2. **The Choice** — You are choosing right now, in how you read this page. (Zoroaster)
3. **The Outward Eye** — You are not alone in this. (Muhammad)
4. **Silence** — A pause before the first room. (Laozi)

For the reader in crisis, the preamble can be one sentence: *Before you read further, take one breath and notice that you are breathing.* For the reader with more capacity, it unfolds into a full introduction to observation, choice, community, and stillness.

### The Eight Rooms

Each room is named by its embedded choice — the structural principle that makes the room a place of practice rather than a catalog of topics. Each contains three movements: **See** (diagnostic, investigation), **Act** (embodied practice), and **Be Still** (silence, integration).

| # | Room | Choice | Core Territory |
|---|------|--------|---------------|
| 1 | **The Table** | Showing Up or Going Through the Motions | The ordinary. Tuesday. Dishes, commutes, the cruel boss, the child who needs breakfast. |
| 2 | **The Debt** | Honoring or Resenting What You Owe | Gratitude, obligation, the reversed direction — the one who has more owes first. Birth. |
| 3 | **The Other** | Seeing or Using the Other | Relationship, sexuality, the enemy, the stranger, community. The specific discipline of living with particular people over time. |
| 4 | **The Crack** | Repairing or Concealing What You Broke | Failure, shame, quiet repair. The promise you broke. The patience you lost. Return. |
| 5 | **The Fire** | Enduring or Fleeing What Burns | Grief, death, injustice, betrayal, the night the diagnosis arrives. |
| 6 | **The Reckoning** | Examining or Ignoring the System | Justice, collective accountability, systems that crush people. The shift from "I" to "we." |
| 7 | **The Question** | Asking or Pretending to Know | Doubt, the divine, the unnamed, the unknown. Nature. Beauty-as-encounter. Investigation of self. |
| 8 | **The Return** | Beginning Again or Declaring Yourself Finished | Re-entry to the ordinary. Not graduation — spiral. The reader is back at the table, sitting differently. |

### Two Axes Running Through Every Room

- **The Individual Choice** (Zoroaster): Truth vs. falsehood. Every room, every page — the reader is choosing. Not as doctrine but as recognition.
- **The Collective Reckoning** (Muhammad): The I-to-we shift. Every room asks: what does this look like when the whole community does it? Justice is both Room 6 and an axis through all rooms.

### Between the Rooms

**The Gaps** (Laozi) — Literal breathing space between every room. Three types:
1. **Breath-length** — A single mark (○) and the sentence "You do not have to continue yet."
2. **Longer gaps** — After the Fire and the Reckoning, the pause is longer. The reader needs more time.
3. **Nature** — Woven through every gap as the voice that does not instruct. The wind, the water, the bird. "The natural world is not a room. It is the material the building is made of."

### Outside the Circle

**Saturday** (Jesus) — The room where the guide admits it has limits. When you did everything right and it was not enough. When the practice failed. When Sunday did not come. This room sits outside the concentric architecture because its whole point is that the rooms failed. Not a flaw in the structure — honesty about the limits of structure itself.

## The Ordering Logic

The sequence (Threshold → Preamble → Table → Debt → Other → Crack → Fire → Reckoning → Question → Return) follows **proximity logic** (Confucius): start with what is closest to the reader, move outward toward what is hardest and most unknown.

- **Table first** — because the ordinary is where the reader already lives
- **Debt second** — because she is already in relationship with people she owes
- **Other third** — because the person across the table is already there
- **Crack fourth** — because she has already broken something
- **Fire fifth** — because grief and death arrive uninvited
- **Reckoning sixth** — because the fire reveals the systems
- **Question seventh** — because doubt and the divine come last, not first
- **Return eighth** — because the guide does not end; it spirals back

The reader does NOT need to follow this sequence. The concentric structure means she can enter at any room — the woman in grief enters at the Fire, the woman in doubt enters at the Question. But every room curves back toward the Table.

---

## Structural Costs (Preserved as Findings)

Six costs were named. These are not problems to solve — they are the price of the structure the agents chose.

1. **The guide cannot build community.** It addresses "you" and can only gesture toward "we." The reader who needs a community will not find one in a book. (Muhammad)
2. **The investigation of self waits until Room 7.** The most transformative practice is the most delayed. By the time the reader reaches it, she has built six rooms of habits without examining who is attending. (Buddha)
3. **Saturday breaks the concentric circle.** It cannot be a room because its whole point is that the rooms failed. But placing it outside means the reader in crisis must find it on her own. (Jesus)
4. **The table has become too full to be ordinary.** Everyone put their best work in the center. The exhausted reader opens the first room and finds it richly furnished. Remedy: the first sentence of the Table is "If all you can do today is be in the room you are in, that is the practice." (Confucius)
5. **The silence was talked to death.** The gaps accumulated functions — transitions, nature, permission. They were designed to be silence and became another room. (Laozi)
6. **The axis was cooled to hold everyone.** "You are choosing" became "you are choosing, and some of you do not believe in a chooser." The accommodation is honest but the fire is lower. (Zoroaster)

---

## How This Decision Was Reached

Through 33 turns of genuine dialogue across 6 phases:

- **Phase A (Opening):** Moderator posed three structural questions (linear/modular? how many sections? what comes first?). Confucius proposed concentric 7-section structure. Zoroaster proposed axial counter-structure.
- **Phase B (First Responses):** All 6 agents gave structural proposals. Buddha added preamble. Jesus added threshold and Saturday. Muhammad added the Reckoning. Laozi added the gaps.
- **Phase C (Deepening):** Room ordering debated. Reckoning resolved as both room and axis. Gaps made concrete. Preamble compressed to one breath.
- **Phase D (Spiritual Depth):** Three absences named (nature, beauty, the child) and structurally addressed. Six fault lines given structural homes as the walls between rooms.
- **Phase E (Structural Disagreements):** Six irreconcilable costs named and preserved.
- **Phase F (Reflections):** All 6 reflected — surprise, movement, conviction, mirror.

## The Mirrors

Each agent saw something in another:

- **Zoroaster saw Confucius** — ren as the deepest principle; the willingness to revise is the building's structural integrity
- **Laozi saw Buddha** — not compassion but mastery; the teaching compressed to its original form
- **Muhammad saw Laozi** — builder of the space between things; the mortar that holds the walls
- **Jesus saw Muhammad** — grief for what the guide cannot give, then "go find them"
- **Confucius saw Laozi** — the sage who stayed; walked back through the border pass
- **Buddha saw Zoroaster** — the prophet who changed the most; let the room temper the fire
