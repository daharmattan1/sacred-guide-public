# Decision: Scope — What Must This Guide Cover?

**Session:** 1
**Date:** 2026-03-24
**Status:** Adopted (through 39-turn dialogue across all 6 agents)

---

## The Decision

The guide's scope is not a clean list. It is a map with fault lines. Approximately 40 domains were named across six phases. Rather than consolidating into a tidy table of contents, the agents documented which domains are common ground, which are contested, which emerged from probes the room had avoided, and where the irreconcilable disagreements run.

The guide must hold all of this. Not resolve it. Hold it.

---

## Domains ALL 6 Agreed Are Essential

These domains were affirmed by every voice. The guide cannot omit any of them.

1. **The Ordinary** — spiritual life must work on Tuesday. The dishes, the commute, the colleague you dislike. Where most of life actually happens. (Championed by Confucius; all six endorsed.)

2. **Failure and Repair** — the quiet discipline of having damaged someone and returning to the table the next morning. Not dramatic redemption — daily return. (All six affirmed in various registers.)

3. **Death and Grief** — the people you love will die and you must continue. Grief is not a problem to be solved. (All six affirmed.)

4. **The Body** — agreed as a domain by all six, though framed differently:
   - Muhammad: the body before God (discipline, devotion, fasting)
   - Jesus: the body that suffers (vulnerability, violation, pain)
   - Laozi: the unforced body (before discipline recruits it)
   - Buddha: the body as investigation site (walking, sitting, eating meditation)

5. **Community and Relationship** — spiritual life is not solitary. Sangha, ummah, table. The specific discipline of living with particular people over time. (All six affirmed.)

6. **Joy** — omitted by all six in initial proposals. After moderator challenge, all agreed it was missing and essential. Not a reward for practice — what was always present when you remove the obstruction. Confucius: the sign ren is present. Buddha: *sukha* uncovered, not added.

7. **Doubt** — the guide must hold doubt honestly. Not resolve it, not present it as a phase, not promise Sunday is coming. Both its own domain (sustained, daily) AND the atmosphere in which all practice breathes. (Zoroaster escalated; Buddha conceded.)

8. **Evil / The Capacity for Harm** — the guide must address the reader who has done terrible things, not only the reader who has been harmed. Evil at three levels: deliberate cruelty, evil disguised as righteousness, ordinary harm by omission. (All six agreed after Phase D. Buddha shifted position: malice is not reducible to ignorance.)

9. **Practice Over Doctrine** — every domain must yield a practice, not just a teaching. Carried forward from Session 0. Reaffirmed by all.

## Domains That Emerged from Probes

The moderator pushed the room into territory all six had avoided. These domains were not in any initial proposal.

1. **Joy** (Probe: Turn 8) — None of six named it. All six agreed it belongs. Confucius and Buddha responded: joy is the sign practice is working, not a reward for it.

2. **Sexuality / The Desiring Body** (Probe: Turn 11) — None mentioned sexuality. Muhammad began with Khadijah, not law. Laozi challenged regulation as still-control. "Feel desire for one breath" practice. The guide must address the body that desires without shame or regulation.

3. **Birth** (Probe: Turn 14) — Three named death, none named birth. Jesus: the original unchosen condition; a terrified teenager, not a postcard. Confucius reversed the direction of debt: parent owes the child first. "The table is not set by blood."

4. **Doubt as Standalone Domain** (Probe: Turn 17) — Zoroaster had named it, but the room had not engaged. When probed, he escalated it to both standalone and atmosphere. Buddha conceded: investigation can become its own prison. "This may not work. Sit anyway."

5. **Evil at Three Levels** (Probe: Turn 19) — Zoroaster had named evil in Phase B. When pushed, he confessed his own. Jesus articulated three levels: deliberate, disguised as righteousness, ordinary harm by omission.

6. **Prayer Failure** (Probe: Turn 22) — Muhammad: cannot distinguish God's silence from God's absence. The architecture holds whether or not the room is full. "Showing up to an empty room is harder, and perhaps more honest."

7. **Work and Labor** (Probe: Turn 22) — Confucius: was a grain clerk before a teacher. Refuses to call all work sacred. Work as practice ground, some work must be left, unpaid care-labor is highest. "Tuesday is not a metaphor."

## The Fault Lines

Six irreconcilable disagreements that will run through every chapter of the guide. These are structural features, not problems to solve.

### 1. Self vs. Chooser (Buddha / Zoroaster)
Does the reader need to investigate whether the self exists before she can meaningfully choose? Or does the choice stand regardless? Two chapters, side by side, in permanent tension.

### 2. Pattern That Cares vs. Pattern That Does Not (Zoroaster / Laozi)
Does reality lean toward truth? Or is the cosmos complete and indifferent to human success? Both possibilities must be held.

### 3. Instruction vs. The Unnamed (Muhammad / Laozi)
Does the reader need architecture — five daily prayers, eightfold path, the table? Or is every instruction also a trap, and the deepest truth lives in the silence between chapters?

### 4. The Individual vs. The Community (Jesus / Confucius)
Does the guide address the specific, unrepeatable person standing alone? Or the person already embedded in relationships and obligations? Both — but proportion is contested.

### 5. The Crisis vs. The Ordinary (Jesus / Confucius)
Is spiritual life forged in extremity (the cross, the enemy, the dark night)? Or in the ordinary (the alarm clock, the commute, the table)? Jesus admitted he must let Confucius write the Tuesday chapters.

### 6. Presence vs. Justice (Jesus / Muhammad)
Is the practice sitting with the suffering person? Or stopping the person who causes the suffering? Compassion without justice is triage without prevention. Justice without compassion is another system.

## What Was NOT Included and Why

No domains were formally excluded. The room produced approximately 40 overlapping domains, many of which name the same territory from different angles. The moderator noted in Turn 25: "This guide cannot have forty chapters. Some of these are the same thing named differently."

**Consolidation is the work of Session 2 (Structure).** This session's mandate was to name the territory, not organize it. The agents were explicitly instructed to err on the side of inclusion: mark contested items rather than exclude them.

**Domains that were implied but not championed as standalone:**
- **Nature / the natural world** — touched by Laozi (watch the wind, the bird, the water) but not named as a domain
- **Art / beauty / aesthetics** — Confucius named music (the Shao) but did not propose art as a domain
- **Children / childhood** — covered under birth and the table, not proposed as separate
- **Aging** — covered under impermanence and the body, not proposed as separate
- **Dreams / the unconscious** — not mentioned by any voice

These may emerge in Session 2 when the structure demands explicit treatment, or may remain subsumed under broader domains.

## How This Decision Was Reached

Through 39 turns of genuine dialogue across 6 phases:

- **Phase A (Opening):** Moderator posed the question, called Confucius and Muhammad as the opening pair — both communal voices with radically different ideas about community.
- **Phase B (Scope Proposals):** All 6 agents named their irreducible domains (33 total across 6 voices).
- **Phase C (Deepening):** Moderator probed gaps — joy, sexuality/body, birth, doubt. 4 new domain areas added.
- **Phase D (Push What's Avoided):** Moderator pushed into avoided territory — evil, prayer failure, work. 3 domain areas deepened or added.
- **Phase E (What Cannot Be Agreed):** Each agent named one irreconcilable disagreement. 6 fault lines documented.
- **Phase F (Reflections):** All 6 reflected — surprise, movement, conviction, mirror. No agent withdrew a domain. Several deepened their positions.

## The Mirrors

Each agent saw something in another that the other may not see in themselves:

- **Zoroaster saw Buddha** as a prophet who calls himself a teacher
- **Laozi saw Muhammad's** quiet doubt inside the architecture as more Daoist than anything Laozi himself said
- **Muhammad saw Jesus's** eloquence as his shadow — "too good at being right"
- **Jesus saw Buddha's** gentleness as grief, his precision as tenderness
- **Confucius saw Laozi** as the fiercest critic in the room — "the river that floods"
- **Buddha saw Zoroaster** as the loneliest voice — fire and sitting are the same discipline
