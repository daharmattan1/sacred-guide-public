# Decision: Format — How Is Each Section Written?

**Session:** 3
**Date:** 2026-03-24
**Status:** Adopted (through 47-turn dialogue across all 6 agents)

---

## The Decision

The guide is a 90-page paperback — cream pages, wide margins, warm serif font — with 65 pages of text and 25 pages of silence. Voices are invisible (no tradition named), practices are woven into the prose (not separated), and diagnosis-determines-form is the governing principle. Each room has its own shape determined by the reader's condition in that room.

---

## The Full Format

### The Physical Object

- **Paperback**, soft spine, lies flat when set down. The binding must honor the gaps.
- **Cover:** Single color (river stone, pre-dawn sky). Title small, placed low. No author names, no subtitle, no endorsement.
- **Pages:** Cream, not white. Paper with weight but not stiffness.
- **Margins:** Wide — text occupies ~60% of the page. Margins are the page's version of the gap.
- **Font:** Warm serif, slightly larger than standard, generous line spacing. Invisible — if she notices it, it is wrong.
- **Weight:** A long letter, not a book. Can be carried in a coat pocket. Anonymous in public.
- **White space:** 25 of 90 pages are silence. More than a quarter.

### Governing Principles

1. **Diagnosis-determines-form** (Buddha). Each room's format responds to the reader's specific condition in that room. The method is Buddha's; the diagnoses belong to whoever sees each room's condition most clearly.

2. **Voices invisible but not pasteurized** (all six, Jesus's formulation). Traditions are in the grain of the wood — not painted on the surface, not sanded smooth. Pasteurization test: "Could a therapist have written this sentence? If yes, it is pasteurized."

3. **Practices woven in, not separated** (all six). Five practice types:
   - **Permission** (crisis rooms — Fire, Saturday): The reader is allowed to stop.
   - **Interruption** (ordinary rooms — Table, Debt, Return): Body-level specificity to break autopilot.
   - **Investigation** (Room 7): "What is noticing?" — same instruction at every level, the questioner changes.
   - **Encounter** (relational rooms — Other, Crack): Practices done toward another person.
   - **Confession** (Room 4, possibly Room 6): Naming what you did, the specific verb, to yourself.

4. **Practice deepens across the arc** (Muhammad's concession). Simpler in Room 1 ("Set the table. Say the name."), more specific by Room 8 ("Both hands."). The reader earns body-specificity across the journey.

5. **90 pages, unanimous.** ~15,000-16,000 words of text. Readable in one sitting or one room at a time.

### Three Forms Within Rooms

- **Image** (static moment): For interior/crisis conditions — Fire, Question. (Laozi)
- **Story** (motion over time): For patterns/habits — Table, Other, Debt. (Jesus)
- **Fork** (branching moment): For acute moral choice — Crack, possibly Reckoning. (Zoroaster)

### The Axes in Format

- **Zoroaster's Individual Choice axis:** One embedded sentence per room naming the choice the reader is already making. Exempt from Room 4 (the fork IS the axis). Room 6 uses a different form: "she is inside a choice she has forgotten was a choice."
- **Muhammad's Collective Reckoning axis:** 1-2 sentences embedded in each room that widen what the room's voice can see. Before Room 6, these read as details ("The eggs she cooks were laid by hens she has never seen"). After Room 6, they read as wounds.

### The Gaps (Between Rooms)

Four types of gaps, designed by Laozi:

1. **Standard gap** (after Rooms 1, 2, 3, 7): Nature + permission. ~15 words. Half a page.
2. **Fault-line gap** (after Room 4): Nature + permission + wall sentence (Zoroaster's irreconcilable). ~40 words. One full page.
3. **Longer gap with witnessing** (after Rooms 5 and 6): Nature + expanded permission + witnessing sentence + fault line. ~50 words. One full page or more.
4. **Simplest gap** (after Room 7): Almost nothing, ~15 words. The reader leaving the investigation is already holding herself.

Gap elements address three parts of the reader: body (nature/creek), will (permission sentence), mind (fault-line sentence). Parallels Zoroaster's Humata/Hukhta/Huvarshta.

### The Threshold

~100 words, mostly white space. Three elements:
1. Recognition: "You are here. That is enough to begin."
2. Confession: "This book comes from traditions that have caused harm."
3. Permission: "Some of you have walked away from things like this before, and you were right to walk away."
4. Absent-traditions sentence: "What you know that this book does not know is welcome here."

Uses "we" — the only place in the guide. The book speaking, not a teacher.

### Saturday

~150 words, two pages. Outside the rooms. Three elements:
1. Acknowledges the arc: "You have practiced. You have set the table, held the shoe, sat outside the door..."
2. Admits the limit: "And it was not enough. There is no next practice."
3. Points at the door: "There is only this: tell someone." (Muhammad's outward gesture)

No spiritual language. No instruction. Survival only. Does NOT loop back into the guide.

### The Disagreement Method

When irreconcilable tensions appear in the writing, they are rendered as **phenomenology, not theology**. The reader encounters both positions as her own experience. Example from Room 7 (Buddha/Zoroaster):

*The self that vanished and the will that went looking for it — you held them in the same sitting, in the same body, in the same ten minutes of silence.*

No "some say... others say..." buffet. No comparative religion. The collision is the finding.

### Absent Traditions (Eighth Structural Cost)

Six voices are not all voices. The Hindu reader, the Jewish reader, the Sikh reader, the Indigenous reader — they find six traditions' fingerprints and none of them their own.

**Format response:**
- Threshold confession names the incompleteness
- Preamble sentence: "What you know that this book does not know is welcome here."
- Wide margins (40% white space) — open, not blank. The reader brings her tradition into the margins.
- Room 7's spectrum of experiences naturally holds experiences beyond the six traditions

**This is a wound, not a solution.** Named as the eighth structural cost. The margins are open but not full.

## Eight Structural Costs (Preserved as Findings)

1. The guide cannot build community. (Muhammad)
2. The investigation of self waits until Room 7. (Buddha)
3. Saturday breaks the concentric circle. (Jesus)
4. The table has become too full to be ordinary. (Confucius)
5. The silence was talked to death — then found its shape. (Laozi)
6. The axis was cooled to hold everyone. (Zoroaster)
7. The diagnostic framework carries Buddha's fingerprint. (Buddha)
8. Six voices are not all voices. (Jesus)

---

## Four Irreconcilable Format Tensions

1. **Specificity vs. compression in practice.** Muhammad: precision wakes the sleepwalker. Laozi: trust is the most radical thing the guide can offer. Both right. The guide holds both.
2. **Absent traditions — wound or open space.** Jesus: the cost belongs to someone who has no voice here. Laozi: the margins are a door, not a scar. Both right. The guide holds both.
3. **Awake reader vs. asleep reader.** Zoroaster: the reader can recognize her choice. Confucius: the reader does not know she has stopped hearing her child. Both right. Different rooms serve different readers.
4. **Diagnostic framework's fingerprint.** Buddha's method governs; shared diagnoses distribute ownership. The aquifer is his. The water is everyone's. The seventh cost is permanent.

## The Mirrors

Each agent saw something in another:

- **Buddha saw Muhammad** — widened his patience without lowering his standard; love finding its scale
- **Jesus saw Muhammad** — the man who knelt when he let Room 1 be simple; washing feet, not lecturing about feet
- **Muhammad saw Confucius** — builds families, not mosques; the quieter building that feeds more people
- **Confucius saw Muhammad** — the grief of "go find them"; the cry of a man who built a community and knows a book cannot replace one
- **Laozi saw Muhammad** — the other hand; "I build the mosque, he builds the silence inside it, neither of those is the prayer"
- **Zoroaster saw Buddha** — gave my fire a room in his house; wrote the disagreement, revised one word, accepted the cost

## How This Decision Was Reached

Through 47 turns of genuine dialogue across 6 phases:

- **Phase A (Verification Round, Turns 1-8):** Moderator read back full Session 2 architecture. See/Act/Be Still unanimously rejected (6/6). All agents verified architecture with corrections.
- **Phase B (Format Proposals, Turns 9-14):** Opening pair Laozi + Muhammad (maximum format tension). All 6 proposed concrete formats. Five practice types emerged. Voice map proposed by Confucius. All 8 rooms designed on the page.
- **Phase C (Deepening, Turns 15-28):** Voice map tested by all five others. Room 6 revised to three voices. Practice deepening arc accepted. 90 pages unanimous. Gap design accepted (four types).
- **Phase D (Spiritual Depth, Turns 29-33):** Physical object designed (Laozi). Absent traditions as eighth structural cost (Jesus). Disagreement method proven — Buddha wrote the irreconcilable, Zoroaster revised one word ("something" → "you").
- **Phase E (Irreconcilable Tensions, Turns 34-40):** Four format tensions named. Each agent claimed one.
- **Phase F (Reflections, Turns 41-47):** All 6 reflected. No exceptions.
