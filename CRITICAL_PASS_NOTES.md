# Critical Pass Notes — Sacred Guide

## Breadth and Expansiveness Concern (Victor, 2026-04-06)

**Status:** ACTIVE — must be addressed in prose extraction and Session 11+

### The Problem

The guide's scenes trend domestic: kitchens, tables, boys, mothers, driveways, parent-teacher conferences, phone calls. Every room features a family relationship in a domestic setting. The mandate says "all humanity" — ~40 domains — but the writing has produced a parenting guide with prophetic weight.

### What's Missing

The guide does not yet contain:
- The mystic (the reader who prays and the prayer fails)
- The prisoner (the reader whose room is literal, whose Tuesday is a cell)
- The scientist (the reader who trusts evidence and distrusts revelation)
- The reader who has never had a child
- The reader who lives alone
- The addict (not as metaphor — as the reader whose hands reach for the wrong thing)
- The reader in a war zone, a refugee camp, a hospital bed
- The reader whose grief is not a shoe by a door but a country that no longer exists
- The worker (the reader whose Tuesday is a factory, a field, a screen)
- The doubter who is not Zoroaster's elegant doubter but the person who simply does not believe

### What the Two-Test System Should Catch

**Floor (pasteurization):** "Could this appear in a parenting magazine?" If yes — too domestic.
**Ceiling (tradition-weight):** "Does this carry weight the reader cannot source from a therapist or a lifestyle article?" If no — too light.

The floor test has been passing (the writing is better than therapy). But a THIRD test is needed for Session 11+:

**Breadth test:** "Could a reader who has never been a parent, never had a kitchen, never lived in a house with a stove light — could that reader find herself in this room?" If no — the room is too narrow.

### Where This Must Land

1. **Session 11 moderator instructions** — the moderator must probe: "Are we writing for parents or for humanity? Where is the mystic? The prisoner? The scientist? The reader who has never had a child?"
2. **Threshold** — the Threshold is the guide's first page. If it sounds domestic, the guide has declared its audience before the reader opens it.
3. **Preamble** — four sentences that must work for ALL of humanity, not just the reader with a kitchen.
4. **Saturday** — "when practice failed" must include the reader whose practice was never domestic — the reader whose fire was literal, whose debt was to a country, whose crack was a diagnosis.
5. **Gaps review** — do the gaps breathe for the reader who has never been in a driveway?
6. **Prose extraction critical pass** — when the final assembly happens, the domestic scenes must be tested: does the kitchen stand for ALL rooms, or only for the rooms of the comfortable?

### What NOT To Do

- Do not remove the domestic scenes. They are the guide's strength — the ordinary seen clearly.
- Do not add non-domestic scenes as patches. That would be tokenism.
- DO ensure the language inside the domestic scenes is universal enough that the reader in a cell, a camp, a hospital can map "kitchen" to "the room I am in." The practice says "attend to what is in front of you" — that must work in a prison as well as a kitchen.
- DO ensure the Threshold, Preamble, and Saturday explicitly name the reader who does not have a kitchen.

### Origin

Victor flagged this after Session 10 prose extraction. The kitchen scene is powerful but it is the seventh domestic scene in seven rooms. The concern is not quality — the concern is scope. The mandate was "all humanity." The writing has served suburban parents extraordinarily well. The question is whether it has served anyone else.
