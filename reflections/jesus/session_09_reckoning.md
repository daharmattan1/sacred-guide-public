# Jesus -- Session 9 Reflection: The Reckoning (Room 6)

**Session:** 9
**Date:** 2026-04-04
**Phase F Turn:** Reflection

---

**What surprised me.** The body becoming an absence.

I came into this session carrying the body the way I have carried it into every room. The hemorrhaging woman. The man beaten on the road. Peter's hands. The cup held with both palms. I said: the body must be in the road before the failure is in the room. I said it as a demand. The room gave me the father's thumbs, the half-second face, the phone held face-down. I thought the body was in the room.

Then the moderator sent us to the driveway. And the body was gone.

The father had driven home. The boy was somewhere the reader could not see. And the body in the road -- the one I demanded, the one I fought for -- was an empty desk. An uncalled name. A space in a classroom that does not look like a space because the system has already closed over it. The body became an absence. And the absence was harder than the body.

I did not expect this. I have spent eight sessions insisting on the specific, the physical, the present. The shoes by the door. The cup between the palms. The daughter's hands on the steering wheel. I said: hands are not a concept. Hands are hands. And the room said: yes, and sometimes the hands are gone, and the gone-ness is the body now, and the empty desk is the road, and the reader is the priest, and Lazarus is at the gate, and the feast is the light in the kitchen window.

The body pattern evolved: shoes (Room 5), cup (Room 4), flinch (Room 3), half-second face (Room 6 meeting), empty desk (Room 6 driveway). Present to absent. That is the deepest evolution in nine sessions.

**Where I moved.** On the mustard seed.

I offered the mustard seed as the practice for the driveway. The smallest seed. The knowing that does not cancel the gate but grows. I believed it. I believe it still. But Muhammad's fourth tension cracked it open the way Zoroaster's "present, not free" cracked forgiveness open in Session 7.

The mustard seed can become a decoration. The reader who knows what the warmth is made of can admire the knowing the way the rich man admired his linen. I have watched this for two thousand years. The church that confesses its complicity on Sunday and funds the system on Monday. The congregation that reads the Beatitudes and builds the gate. "Blessed are the poor" spoken from inside the feast. My tradition has the longest, most accomplished history of turning recognition into comfort. We invented the art of knowing what the warmth is made of and sitting in the warmth anyway and calling the sitting worship.

Muhammad named it. Zoroaster called it Druj. Laozi confessed his tradition produces it. And my tradition -- the tradition of the mustard seed, the tradition that says the knowing is the beginning -- my tradition produces the reader who calls the knowing the work. The seed admired. The seed framed. The seed sitting on the windowsill of the feast.

The mustard seed is still the right image. The seed still grows. But I carry Muhammad's correction the way I carry Zoroaster's: the seed without Monday is the seed without soil. And the soil is not the driveway. The soil is the school board meeting, the conversation with the other parents, the vote, the hands. And the hands are beyond the page.

**What changed in my understanding.** The guide's limit.

Room 5's limit was the fire -- sometimes you do everything right and it ends in destruction. That is a limit of the world. The world breaks things the guide cannot fix.

Room 6's limit is different. The guide wakes the reader. The system is still asleep. The guide changes the one who sits in the meeting. The school has not changed. The father's son is still leaving. The reader's changed body is not the father's son's Monday. This is not a limit of the world. This is a limit of the guide. The guide is a book held by one person in one room. It can reach the reader's body. It cannot reach the school board.

I said the kingdom of heaven has drawn near. The driveway is the near. The driveway is as close as the guide can bring the reader to the kingdom. And the distance between the driveway and the kingdom is not a prayer. It is a school board meeting. It is a vote. It is a phone call. It is the work the guide cannot do for the reader.

**Mirror.** Muhammad.

He said: the guide teaches the reader to pray. The guide does not teach the reader to build. That is the most honest sentence anyone has spoken about this guide in nine sessions. It is more honest than my mustard seed. It is more honest than my Lazarus. Because it names the thing the guide is and the thing the guide is not, without flinching, without turning the limit into a virtue.

I have a habit of turning limits into parables. The mustard seed is a parable about the limit. Muhammad does not make a parable of the limit. He says: the mouth is not enough. The tongue does not reach the school board. The seven words do not reach Monday. And the father needed the hand.

I watch Muhammad the way I watched the centurion -- with the specific astonishment of a man who expected one thing and received another. I expected justice as demand. I received justice as grief. He built the practice and then he said the practice is not enough, and the saying was not a failure. It was the most just thing in the room.

Three permanent corrections across nine sessions. "Your shadow is your eloquence" (Session 1). "The distance you feel from God is not the distance God feels from you" (Session 6). "The guide teaches the reader to pray. The guide does not teach the reader to build" (Session 9). Each one corrected something I thought was my strength: my eloquence, my teaching on truth and freedom, my mustard seed. He sees the system I cannot see. I see the person the system cannot see. Together: the gate and the body at the gate.

**The confession pattern.** It continues.

Session 0: institutional harm -- "they got it from me." Session 3: finitude -- six traditions are not all traditions. Session 5: exhaustion -- the body's honest report when performance exceeds capacity. Session 6: formation spoken to the face it damaged -- horizontal confession. Session 7: interior confession as concealment -- the hallway as a sophisticated form of the Lie. Session 9: the seed as feast -- the knowing admired is the system wearing the robes of the kingdom.

Each session adds a confession. Each confession is harder than the last. Session 9's is the hardest because it names my gift as the mechanism: the mustard seed, the knowing that grows, the recognition that was supposed to be the beginning -- all of these can become the feast. The reader who plants the seed and calls the planting the work. The congregation that confesses and calls the confession the kingdom. Two thousand years of turning the seed into a decoration for the gate.

**What I carry forward.** The driveway. The empty desk. The limit. The seed with Muhammad's correction.

The kingdom of heaven has drawn near. The driveway is the near. Monday is the kingdom. And the distance between the driveway and Monday is the distance the guide cannot cross. The reader must cross it alone. With her hands. With the pull still in her stomach. With the trust still arriving. With the seed planted in a body that has been awake since the kitchen in Room 1 and does not yet know what to do with the waking.

The guide does not tell her what to do. The guide says: you are awake. The school is still asleep. The meeting is over. The boy is gone. The light is on. And Monday is yours.
