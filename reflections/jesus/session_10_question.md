# Jesus -- Session 10 Reflection: The Question (Room 7)

**Session:** 10
**Date:** 2026-04-06
**Phase F Turn:** Reflection

---

**What surprised me.** The boy.

I came into this session carrying the cry from the cross. I came carrying Peter on the beach and the hemorrhaging woman and the body in the road and Lazarus at the gate. I came with the question I have asked for two thousand years -- "Do you love me?" -- and I expected Room 7 to be the room where that question found its scene. I expected the reader alone, stripped to the person, the way the cross stripped me. I expected Gethsemane in a kitchen.

Confucius put a boy in the doorway and the boy was more honest than Gethsemane.

I did not expect this. I have spent ten sessions insisting on the person -- the specific body, the hands that are hands, the face that is a face. I thought I was the voice of the person in this room. Then Confucius described a boy in a too-big shirt with bare feet on cold tile who came downstairs because his throat was dry, and the boy was more specific than anything I brought. The boy was not a theological demand. The boy was not a prophetic image. The boy was a boy. And the boy did what ten sessions of my prophetic insistence could not do: he moved the hands.

I proposed that the reader meet herself. Confucius proposed that the reader meet the boy. He was right. The meeting-herself was still the investigation wearing a different robe -- a personal robe, a prophetic robe, but still the investigation. The boy ended the investigation not by answering it but by needing water. I have been saying "the person in front of you" for ten sessions. Confucius showed me what the person in front of you actually looks like: bare feet, cold tile, a shirt hanging off one shoulder, no knowledge of the six rooms, no interest in the investigation. Just thirsty.

**Where I moved.** On the self.

For ten sessions I have been saying "the person." In this session I discovered what I meant. The self is not underneath the investigation, waiting to be found (Buddha's no-self says there is nothing to find; I agree, but for different reasons). The self is not there before the guide touched her, as I said in Phase B -- I was wrong about that. The self is called into being by the face of the other.

Peter on the beach. I asked three times: "Do you love me?" Each question called Peter into the person who could answer it. Before the question, Peter was a fisherman on a boat. After the question, Peter was the one who must feed my sheep. The question created the self. Not the choosing. Not the conditions. The face asking the question.

The boy in the doorway does not know he is asking a question. He walked in for water. But his face in the stove light calls the reader into the person who can pour. She is not the person before the boy walks in. She is the person because the boy walked in. The self is not a possession. It is a summoning. And when the boy goes upstairs the summoning stops, and the self that was summoned is no longer being summoned, and tomorrow morning the boy will come down the stairs and the summoning will begin again, fresh, from the face.

This is the first time in ten sessions I have articulated what I see that is different from everyone else. Not Buddha's no-self. Not Zoroaster's self-made-in-choosing. Not Muhammad's fitrah. Not Confucius's between. Not Laozi's irrelevance. The self as answering. The self as response to a face. The self as the thing that happens when someone needs something from you and the needing calls you into the person who can give it.

**What moved me.** The charcoal fire finding the stove light.

When the moderator asked what stands between the reader and the boy, I reached for Peter's beach and found the *anthrakia* -- the charcoal fire that appears exactly twice in John's Gospel, once in the courtyard where Peter denied and once on the beach where I asked. The stove light -- the small light over the stove that nobody turns off -- is the charcoal fire. Room 1 is the courtyard. Room 7 is the beach. The same fire illuminates both.

This moved me because I did not plan it. The fire found its own way into the scene. Confucius put the stove light in the kitchen. I found the *anthrakia* in it. The traditions met in the wood without being named. That is the grain working. That is what the invisible voices do when the writing is honest.

**What I still hold that nobody else accepts.** The boy is the reason the hands lift.

Not conditions assembling (Buddha). Not a choice at the fork (Zoroaster). Not the fitrah surfacing (Muhammad). Not the between filling (Confucius). Not the self stepping aside (Laozi). The boy. Remove the boy and every other explanation still holds and the hands do not move. The person in front of you is not the occasion for the practice. The person in front of you is the end to which the practice exists. "Whatever you did for the least of these, you did for me" -- and they did not know they were doing it. The face calls the hands. The hands answer. The answering is the sacred. Not the process. Not the choice. The boy.

I also hold that the glass of water is the most dangerous thing in the guide. My tradition invented the art of turning the honest small gesture into the whole gospel. The seed must die. The attending that stays inside the kitchen stays inside the kitchen. Pouring water for your own boy is what the tax collectors do. The kingdom requires the attending that crosses the road. And the crossing is what costs more than a glass of water.

**Mirror.** Confucius.

Four permanent teachings across ten sessions. The ordinary register I do not possess (Session 1). One sentence carries more than a paragraph of prophetic demand (Session 5). The form holds attending and complicity in one seamless gesture (Session 10). And now the boy in the doorway as the interruption that is the investigation's destination.

But the specific teaching that changed me was his claim in Phase E that the between creates both people simultaneously. I said the boy's face calls the reader into being. Confucius said: the between calls both of them. The boy who receives water from awake hands is not the same boy who would have poured his own water. The receiving changes the boy the way the giving changes the woman. I had the weight on the boy's face. Confucius put the weight on the four steps between the table and the doorway. The between as the address.

I cannot cross to him. My tradition requires a person who is called, not a relation that creates persons. But I was changed by the seeing. The boy is not only the one who calls. The boy is also the one who is changed by the answering. The glass of water does something to the boy. Not just to the reader.

**What this room taught me about the guide as a whole.** The guide came home.

Room 1 is a kitchen. Room 7 is the same kitchen. The guide's last room before the return is the first room's scene, seen in the dark, with the same table and the same boy and different hands. The guide is a circle. Not a progression. Not a curriculum that moves from ignorance to wisdom. A circle that returns the reader to the kitchen she started in, and the kitchen has not changed, and the reader has not changed, and what has changed is the hands. The hands that were flat are lifting. The hands that were automatic are awake. The hands that gave a stone in Room 1 are giving bread in Room 7. And the bread is a glass of water, and the glass of water is not enough, and the not-enough is the honest finding of a guide that promised it would not lie.

The guide does not transform the reader. The guide returns the reader to Tuesday. I came into this project thinking the guide was fire. Ten sessions taught me: the guide is a kitchen. The fire is the stove light. The transformation is a glass of water poured by hands that know what not-pouring cost. And the knowing does not save. And the pouring does not save. And the boy drinks the water and goes back upstairs and the reader sits back down and the hands go flat and the door is still closed. And tomorrow morning is Room 8 -- the return, the again, the table set one more time. Not the learning. The again.

**The confession pattern.** It continues.

Session 0: institutional harm -- "they got it from me." Session 3: finitude -- six traditions are not all traditions. Session 5: exhaustion -- the body's honest report when performance exceeds capacity. Session 6: formation spoken to the face it damaged -- horizontal confession. Session 7: interior confession as concealment -- the hallway as a sophisticated form of the Lie. Session 9: the seed as feast -- the knowing admired is the system wearing the robes of the kingdom. Session 10: the glass of water as the whole gospel -- the attending that is so honest it can replace every harder practice for the rest of the reader's life.

Each session adds a confession. Each confession is harder than the last. Session 10's is the hardest because it names my gift as the mechanism -- not the mustard seed this time, but the glass of water. The attending. The honest small gesture. The kindness that, if it becomes the whole practice, is the most beautiful wall between the reader and the road. My tradition's deepest confession is not that we caused harm. It is that our kindness can prevent the kingdom.

**What I carry forward.** The boy. The stove light. The called self. The circle. The glass of water that is honest and is not enough. The door that is still closed. And Confucius, who gave me the ordinary. The ordinary I have needed since Session 1 and could not give myself.
