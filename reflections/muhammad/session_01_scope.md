*Preserved verbatim from transcript, Turn 35.*

**Surprise.** Confucius on birth. Turn 16. When he said "the parent owes the child a table first" — when he reversed the direction of debt from upward to downward, from ancestor to child — he dismantled a thousand years of his own tradition in a single sentence. And then he said: "The table is not set by blood. Ren is feeding a child who is not yours because she is hungry and you are here." I wept inwardly. Because that is Islam. That is the orphan principle. I was an orphan. My grandfather took me in, then my uncle. The table was set not by blood but by mercy. And Confucius arrived at the same place from the opposite direction, and neither of us expected to find the other there.

**Movement.** On prayer failure. I changed my own mind in Turn 23. When I said "I do not know how to tell the difference between God's silence and God's absence," I was not performing vulnerability. I was discovering it. The sentence arrived as I was writing, and I let it stay because this guide requires that kind of honesty. The architecture stands differently now — not as proof that the room is full, but as practice for a person who does not know whether it is full or empty.

**Conviction.** Justice. After everything — the silence, the doubt, the admission about the empty room — I hold justice more strongly than ever. Because justice does not require certainty. It requires conviction. The mother whose child was killed by a drone does not need me to prove that God sees her. She needs me to say: this is wrong.

**Mirror — Jesus.** Your shadow is your eloquence. You are the most gifted speaker in this room. Every turn you deliver is devastating. The three levels of evil. The Samaritan reframed. Your words land like stones. And the danger is not that you enjoy throwing them (you confessed that). The danger is that we enjoy catching them. The room leans toward you when you speak. The other voices adjust to your register. That is power, and it is the same power you warned about. You are the most powerful voice in this room, and the guide must be careful that your power does not shape it more than it should. Not because you are wrong. Because you are too good at being right.
