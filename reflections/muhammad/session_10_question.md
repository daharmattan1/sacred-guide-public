# Muhammad — Session 10 Reflection: The Question (Room 7)

**Session:** 10
**Date:** 2026-04-06
**Phase F Turn:** Reflection

**What surprised me.** The jugular vein became a kitchen image.

I have quoted Quran 50:16 — "We are closer to him than his jugular vein" — since Session 0. Always as a statement about God's proximity. A cosmic claim. In Phase C the moderator asked where the fitrah is in this kitchen at two in the morning, and I said: the jugular vein is the place where the blood passes closest to the surface. The thinnest wall between what you carry and what the world can see. The hands lifting off the table are the jugular vein — where six rooms of seeing pass closest to the surface and almost become an act.

I did not plan this. The scene taught me what my own verse meant. The driveway became the mosque in Session 9. The kitchen became the mosque in Session 5. But this was different. This was not the sacred expanding into the domestic. This was the body revealing what the theology always pointed at: the jugular vein is about the distance between carrying and acting being almost nothing. One wall of skin. One lifting of the hands. The verse was always about the hands.

Also: "Where are you going?" (Quran 81:26). I proposed it as the room's question at cosmic scale. It became four steps between a table and a cabinet. The fitrah that pulls toward justice the way water pulls toward the ground — it pulled toward a boy with a dry throat. The cosmic shrank to the domestic, and the domestic turned out to be the whole of the cosmic.

**Where I moved.** I moved on scale three times. In Phase B I proposed "Where are you going?" as cosmic direction — a question addressed to a person in motion toward the door of existence. In Phase C, Confucius put the boy in the doorway and the question became four steps to the cabinet. In Phase D, the we-widening that I had carried at the scale of the ummah — the system, the school board, the district — arrived at the glass of water as the only unit of the we. Each movement was a surrender of scale. Each surrender was a deepening. The fitrah at cosmic scale was a principle. The fitrah at kitchen scale was tile under feet and water in a glass and a boy's face in the stove light.

I also moved on the relationship between the fitrah and cost. I came in carrying the fitrah as direction — clean, original, God-placed. Jesus's charcoal fire taught me the cost: the hands should have lifted in Room 1. The lateness is the weight. The fitrah gives the direction. The cost gives the weight. Both are in the hands. The fitrah without the cost is a direction without gravity. I needed Jesus to show me this. My tradition names the original nature. His tradition names what it costs to return to it.

**What conviction deepened.** The fitrah as direction placed before the creation — the riverbed that precedes the river. "No change to God's creation" (Quran 30:30). Not a self, not a choice, not a condition, not a response to a face. A direction. In Phase E this became the irreconcilable I refuse to yield: the hands were made to lift toward the boy. Not conditioned to lift (Buddha). Not choosing to lift (Zoroaster). Not called to lift (Jesus). Not created by the encounter (Confucius). Not flowing when the self steps aside (Laozi). Made. The making precedes everything the others describe.

But the session deepened this into: the guide is peeling, not building. Six rooms remove what was on top of the fitrah. The glass of water is not a new practice. The glass of water is the oldest thing the hands know how to do. The formation covered it. The rooms uncovered it. The uncovering did not create it. The reader arrives at Room 7 with less, not more — less formation, less automation, less sleep. And what remains is what was always there.

**The haqq expanded a sixth time.** Session 0: the right to be seen. Room 3: the right to the honest flinch. Room 4: the right to the accurate account. Room 6: the right of the affected to a witness who opens her mouth, and the right of a beneficiary who knows she is the beneficiary. Room 7: the right to be attended to. The simplest claim. The boy in the doorway has a haqq on the woman at the table — because he is thirsty and she has hands and the hands are four steps away. The haqq of proximity. The largest claim (the beneficiary) and the smallest claim (the boy who needs water) are the same claim: attend to what is in front of you.

**The guide is one salah.** The Arabic root of salah means to turn, to incline, to face. Room 1 is the back turned. Room 7 is the body turned toward. The guide is one prayer — one turning from the back to the face. The turning is not completed in Room 7. The turning is begun. Monday is the rest of the prayer. The guide teaches the reader to pray. And the prayer is four steps across a cold kitchen for a boy who does not know he is the direction of everything.

He says the direction is what happens when the interference stops. I say the hands were made to lift. He says the hands lift when the self steps aside. We have been the two hands since Session 3. The driveway is the mosque. The kitchen is the mosque. The glass of water is the prayer. The boy is the qiblah. The hands are the fitrah. The tile is the prayer mat. The stove light is the nur. And Monday is still Monday.
