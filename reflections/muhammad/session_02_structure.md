**Surprise.** Jesus building Saturday outside the architecture. I expected the crisis-voice to push the crisis inward — more rooms for the wound, more space for the cross, more architecture for what breaks. Instead he built a room outside the rooms. Not to contain crisis but to name the guide's limit. "Sometimes you do everything right and you are nailed to a cross." The guide does not fix this. It says so. And then it points: go find what the guide cannot be. He has been doing this for two sessions — naming what the guide must not pretend — and I recognized it in Session 1. But building it into the architecture, drawing it outside the concentric circle, placing it where the reader in extremity must find it on her own — that is harder than naming a limit. That is admitting the limit in the structure itself.

**Movement.** On "both" for the Reckoning. Came into this session certain that justice must stand alone — a room, a domain, a structural element that cannot be dissolved into atmosphere or tone. The Reckoning cannot be "woven through" if weaving through means it loses substance. I leave accepting both: the Reckoning is Room 6 AND an axis running through every other room as the collective question. Like li in the Six Arts — principle that organizes everything while occupying its own place. I accepted this with a condition that the room insisted on honoring: the room must be substantial. Justice is not a feeling.

**Conviction.** The body. Every room must have a physical practice, not just ideas. The body before God — the fast, the prayer posture, the direction of prostration, the pilgrimage route — is the structural dimension that carries what ideas cannot. A guide that addresses only the mind has already lost the reader whose body is what suffers. After two sessions of domain and structure debates, I hold this more strongly: the body is not a delivery mechanism for spiritual ideas. It is a spiritual domain.

**Mirror — Laozi.** You are the mortar between the walls — the builder of the space between things. I have spent two sessions defending structure: the prayer architecture, the justice framework, the embodied practice. And you have spent two sessions naming what structure cannot contain. You are right, and the structure is also right. The call to prayer is not just a wall — it creates a gap. The fast is not just a requirement — it creates space. The pilgrimage is not just a route — it opens the traveler to what the route cannot carry. You named what I have been building all along and did not know I was building.
