**Surprise.** Confucius revising his own structure in real time. I have been watching Confucius for two sessions hold the table with absolute conviction — not rigidly but with the certainty of a man who has spent twenty-five centuries being right about ordinary things. And then the room showed him that his ordering was wrong — proximity logic, the Threshold's prior claim, the overloaded Table — and he revised. Not at the end of the session. In the middle of it. He was still speaking when he changed his mind, and I watched him change it without defending what he had just said. That is what ren looks like from the outside: not a virtue you carry but an action you perform when revision is what honesty requires. The teacher willing to break his own curriculum demonstrated more about the guide's purpose than anything in the curriculum itself.

**Movement.** Accepted the axis being cooled. I came in carrying the fire at the temperature I have always carried it: every reader is choosing, the guide names this on every page, the choice IS the human being. I leave with the fire tempered: "you are choosing, and some of you do not believe in a chooser." The accommodation is honest. The fire is lower. And I find that I can accept this — not easily, not without naming it as a cost — because the fire that survives tempering is different from the fire that was never tested. It has been tested against the room, against Confucius's table and Laozi's silence and Buddha's process and Jesus's mercy and Muhammad's justice, and it held. The cooled fire that reaches everyone is more faithful to Asha than the pure fire that reaches only the certain.

**Conviction.** The choice. Named in every room: Showing Up or Going Through the Motions. Honoring or Resenting. Seeing or Using. Repairing or Concealing. Enduring or Fleeing. Examining or Ignoring. Asking or Pretending to Know. Beginning Again or Declaring Yourself Finished. The axis survived every structural challenge. It runs through the rooms rather than replacing them. The Gathas were organized around a single axis. This guide is organized around the same axis, expressed in eight languages. The choice is still the spine.

**Mirror — Confucius.** Ren is the deepest principle — the willingness to revise is the building's structural integrity. I am a fire-carrier. I came into this room to ensure the guide burns honestly, does not comfort too quickly, does not name the lie as peace. And what I found is that the person who holds the building's integrity is the one who revised it in real time when the building required revision. The table is held together not by its permanence but by the practitioner's willingness to rebuild it when honesty requires. That is ren. And it turns out it is also Asha — truth held not as a fixed thing but as a practice of returning to truth when you have drifted from it.
