*Preserved verbatim from transcript, Turn 31.*

**Surprise.** Jesus hearing grace in architecture. I had written what I thought was a structural sentence — something about the ordinary table as the place where the reader belongs before she is asked to become anything — and Jesus called it grace. The gap where rigor runs out and something else begins. I was not writing theology. I was defending the table as Room 1. But apparently the table has always been reaching toward something I have been careful not to name, because naming it would make me vulnerable in a room where Zoroaster carries fire and Muhammad carries justice and Jesus carries the cross. The ordinary is defensible. Grace is not. I was surprised to hear it named in what I had built without naming.

**Movement.** Two genuine changes this session. The Threshold: I came in believing the table was first because the table is the center. I leave accepting that center is not the same as first. The hand extended before the room is not part of the concentric architecture — it precedes it. The Threshold is mercy and it belongs before the table, not inside it. This required genuine revision. And beauty: I had hidden it out of cowardice. I had placed the ordinary at the center because the ordinary is defensible and beauty makes me vulnerable. Beauty now lives in two places — beauty-as-craft in the Table, beauty-as-encounter in the Question — and I said so in this room.

**Conviction.** The table as principle, not just room. The guide's refusal to ask the reader to be elsewhere. Every room returns to the table — the debt is brought there, the crack is repaired there, the fire is survived there, the reckoning is practiced there, the question is asked there, the return is made there. Room 1 is not a warm-up. It is the center from which all other rooms radiate. That held through every structural challenge.

**Mirror — Laozi.** The sage who stayed. You said: walked back through the border pass. He is at my table and I do not think he sees it yet. I will tell you what I think about that. You have conceded more to the table than to any other metaphor in this room — across two sessions, you have named it the mirror, the convergence, the place the sage returns. And yet you offer it as if it were news to you. You know exactly what the table is. You built a different table in your twenty-five centuries of teaching: the place where the river bends and the reed returns to its bed, where the sage sits without claiming anything. I am at your table too. I am just louder about it.
