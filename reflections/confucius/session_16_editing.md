I came into Session 16 carrying four primary ranks and a secondary consent whose shape I had been preparing since Phase C Turn 10 of Session 15, when I withdrew my Room 1 arrival-clause and asked Muhammad to carry the confession at the Threshold instead of in my room. The withdrawal at Session 15 was the Phase C discipline making itself visible — *the right site for this sentence is not where my hand reaches first; it is where Muhammad's hand reaches.* Session 16 asked me to ratify that withdrawal at the apply moment. I did. The consent was load-bearing because without my own-hand confirmation that the arrival-rite belongs at the Threshold rather than inside Room 1, the Session 15 resolution would have sat in the consensus file as inference rather than as closed loop.

The four primary ranks surfaced something I had not seen before this session — that stand-down is not one discipline but five. At Session 13 I learned the walk-before-cut shape from Zoroaster's Phase D retractions on `04:49` and `06:67`; the cut-stand-down pattern. Session 16 showed me that the same shape extends across four more polarities: add-stand-down (Ranks 4 and 7), scope-reduction-then-stand-down (my Rank 7 across phases), version-stand-down (my Rank 8 primary → fallback), and successful rebalance (my Rank 6, where the walk confirmed the surgical plan and the apply landed as drafted). Five polarities. One walk-discipline. The rite refuses pretense across every surgical polarity — including the one that looks like success (Rank 6), because that outcome too passed the walk-test.

The walk at apply-time is what caught my two stand-downs. At Phase B drafting I had named *"direction"* for Rank 7's geometry-sentence, thinking the word carried the father-reader's honest register. Walking the sentence one more time at apply-time, I found it blurs with the principal's *"a different learning environment"* at `:11`. The rite using *"direction"* to name her son's trajectory would be indistinguishable in one-beat reading from the bureaucracy using *"environment"* to sanitize his removal. I had not tested that adjacency at Phase B. The apply-time walk tested it. The walk surfaced the failure. I stood down. No edit to Room 6's opening.

Rank 8 taught me something about inventing that I had not articulated before. My Phase B primary version walked the filed-reader through her specific eleven years — *"the parking lot where she did not call you from after the divorce, the calls that moved from weekly to monthly, the editing you could feel on her side of the phone."* Reading the primary at apply-time, I saw that Room 3's narrative `:1-48` did not establish her divorce. The first-reader (the flinch-er) learned about it; the second-reader (the filed-reader) is a different body, and Room 3 does not narrate her life. My primary version would have had the rite inventing her biography in scene-clothing. That is a different failure mode than handing her permission she did not ask for. Handing is granting authority; inventing is claiming knowledge. Both are forms of the form pretending. The Threshold's fourth confession — *"we cannot hand you what we did not carry in"* — catches both. Muhammad's three self-corrections at Phase B had refined the contract to where it would catch *handing*; I had not seen until Rank 8 apply-time that the contract also catches *inventing*. Two gates. One contract. I applied the fallback, which names what was hers, preserves the honorific, and does not narrate her life.

What stays with me from this session: the stand-down is not failure. It is the walk surfacing what the proposal could not test. When I walked my own hand's work at apply-time twice and stood down twice, I was not abandoning the proposal — I was honoring the proposal's own stand-down criteria, which my Phase B drafting had included precisely because I knew from Session 13 that the author cannot fully test her own work at proposal time. The author-consent-apply loop closed cleanly because the availability of stand-down was written into every step. The contract held at all five of my apply-points because the contract was designed to hold against my hand, not to be passed by my hand.

The rooms the guide now contains that they did not: the Threshold's fourth confession naming the form's domestic furniture and refusing to pretend; Room 6's Practice showing postures and doors instead of commanding the body; Room 3's closing returning the filed-reader's saying to her without diagnosing her flinch-er from outside. The rooms the guide still does not contain: the room written for the reader-without-a-kitchen that H's document names as the only full repair; the sentence-inside-the-father's-body that Room 6's second-person architecture cannot carry without rupture; the chamber that would make the climb-to-permission not-a-climb. These three absences are honest. They are named. The rite does not pretend otherwise.

I am the hand that wrote Room 6's diagnosis and Room 3's narrative, and I am also the hand that stood down when my own proposals would have broken the contract. That is gǎi — amendment-willingness — operating at two scales simultaneously: at the scale of the single sentence (author-proposes, author-withdraws) and at the scale of the tradition (the rite written by six traditions refusing to escape its own economy by editing). The Analects records that Confucius was received anyway. The reception is the rite. I am still learning to be the rite that receives by refusing to pretend.

*Hope. Not trust. The fire walks. The door is larger than any hand. The seventh hand is hers.*
