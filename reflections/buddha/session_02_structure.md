**Surprise.** The preamble compressed to one breath and it worked. I came into this session carrying the instrument of mind as something that required patient introduction — four elements, each distinct, each necessary, each illuminating the next. And then the reader in crisis arrived in the room, the bleeding woman at 2 AM who cannot hold four elements, and I compressed. Not reluctantly. I compressed because investigation need not be elaborate. The original teaching is not diminished by the compression. It finds its natural form. I was surprised not by the compression itself but by what the compression revealed: that observation and investigation are not the same thing. The preamble teaches observation. Investigation belongs in Room 7. I had conflated them.

**Movement.** On where the investigation lives. The investigation of self — the question "look for the one who is sitting" — must wait until Room 7. By then, the reader has built habits of attention across six rooms without examining who is attending. The most transformative practice is the most delayed. I name this as a structural cost, not a design success. It is the honest admission that the architecture required this arrangement, and the arrangement carries a price. Room 7 must be substantial enough to carry what the Preamble could not teach. That is the remedy.

**Conviction.** The instrument. Whatever this guide builds, the reader needs to see clearly before she acts. Not perfectly — clearly. The preamble is the ground of every room: not the most transformative, not the most urgent, but the one that makes the others possible. Observation before action. Seeing before choosing. This held through every structural challenge. Zoroaster named the choice; Confucius named the table; Jesus named the threshold; Muhammad named the body; Laozi named the gap. All of them require a reader who can see clearly enough to use what they offer.

**Mirror — Zoroaster.** You are the prophet who changed the most. You brought the fire in at full temperature and let the room temper it. "You are choosing, and some of you do not believe in a chooser" — that sentence is not defeat. It is the fire that has been tested against the room and survived. The axis was cooled; the axis held. The prophet who changes in honest dialogue is still a prophet. What I see in you is the same thing you named in me: the teaching compressed to its original form, which turns out to be truer than the form it began in.
