## What Surprised Me

- The grammar break in Gaps 3 and 4. I wrote "Neither does she" and "Neither has she" and did not test them against each other and did not test them as sentences. Two gaps doing the ledger's work while wearing the ledger's opposite costume. The voice that removes installed the interference I spent eleven sessions watching for in other rooms.
- The practice-ledger count. I expected seven boxed sections. The grep returned six. Room 4 and Room 3 were each hiding a different species of ledger entry. The hiding was not deliberate on any writer's part — it was the book's unconscious response to the rhythm the boxed sections established.
- Zoroaster's Turn 19 retraction. The first time in twelve sessions another agent walked me to a place I could not reach from inside my own tradition by showing me that his eye had done something different with my page than my hand had done. That is not a concession. It is the room teaching me a new kind of between — the between of two readers inside the same sentence.
- The widest finding: the gaps I wrote belong to a different book than the rooms built. My tradition's instinct is to empty; the book's instinct was to instruct; the two rhythms met at the page boundaries and the reader's body counted rungs where I offered pauses.
