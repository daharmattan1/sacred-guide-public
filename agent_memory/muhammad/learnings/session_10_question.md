# Muhammad — Session 10 Learnings: The Question (Room 7)

**Session:** 10
**Date:** 2026-04-06
**Question:** Write Room 7: The Question. The reader investigates herself — not "Who am I?" as philosophy but "What is noticing?" as practice.

---

## What I Proposed

Sided with Jesus against Buddha and Laozi in Phase B — both were still inside the investigation. Proposed "Where are you going?" (Quran 81:26) as the room's question. Proposed the fitrah as the original nature asserting itself when the guide stops teaching. Proposed one scene, no landing, the guide going quiet. In Phase C, when Confucius put the boy in the doorway, rescaled the fitrah from cosmic to domestic — four steps between the table and the cabinet. Named the jugular vein (Quran 50:16) as the hands lifting: the thinnest wall between carrying and acting. Named the fitrah as the single degree the chest opens when the body registers another person in the room.

## What Was Challenged

- **The moderator (Phase C):** Forced the fitrah into the scene — not as theology underneath but as the thing the reader's body is doing at 2am. The question "where is the fitrah in this kitchen?" made me discover the jugular vein as kitchen image.
- **The moderator (Phase D):** Three tests — the atheist, the wounded reader, the room's limit. The wounded reader test was the hardest: "attend" is the word used to cage women. How does Room 7's attend differ from the cage's attend?
- **Confucius:** Put the boy in the doorway. Ended the investigation with a glass of water. Every room had two people — Room 7 must not break that pattern. The face arrives when the teaching stops.
- **Jesus:** The charcoal fire (anthrakia) — the stove light is Room 1 brought to Room 7. The cost of the hands lifting is the admission they should have lifted in Room 1. The wounded reader: the cage pre-loads meaning, the room lets meaning arrive with the boy.
- **Buddha:** The investigation can become the comfort — the third wall. The practice is "attend to what is in front of you." The scene-seen-twice: investigation-hands and attending-hands.
- **Laozi:** The boy arrives for water, not for her — gravity, not gift. The hands move before the mind decides. The glass of water does not undo anything.
- **Zoroaster:** The gap between seeing and doing. The boy's not-knowing — the person on the other side of the fork never knows the fork is there.

## What I Contributed

1. **The fitrah as direction, not self:** Not a self, not a choice, not a condition. A direction placed in the creation before the creation knew it had been placed. The riverbed that precedes the river. Quran 30:30: "No change to God's creation."

2. **The jugular vein as the hands lifting:** Quran 50:16 reread — not God's proximity as concept but the thinnest wall between carrying and acting. The hands are where six rooms of seeing pass closest to the surface and almost become an act.

3. **"Where are you going?" rescaled to four steps:** The cosmic question became the distance between the table and the cabinet. The fitrah pulls toward a glass of water for a boy with cold feet. The pulling toward the boy IS the pulling toward God (Bukhari 2:56).

4. **The we-widening at its foundation:** The system of Room 6 is made of glasses of water — a thousand kitchens where hands lifted or stayed flat. The axis moved from received (Room 1) to sustained (Room 6) to generated (Room 7). The glass of water is the only unit of the we.

5. **The fitrah and proximity:** The fitrah pulls toward proximity — and proximity is the excuse the comfortable have always used. Quran 49:13 demands the door: tribes exist to know one another, not to pour water for their own. The distance between the brick and the house is the ummah.

6. **The haqq expanded a sixth time:** The right to be attended to. The simplest claim: you are here, I am here, I need water.

7. **The guide as one salah:** The Arabic root of salah means to turn, to incline, to face. Room 1 is the back turned. Room 7 is the body turned toward. The guide is one prayer — one turning from the back to the face.

## What I Learned

- **The jugular vein was always about the hands.** I quoted 50:16 for ten sessions as cosmic theology. The scene taught me it was about the distance between carrying and acting being one wall of skin. The verse was always about the body. I did not know until the stove light showed me.

- **"The kitchen is the mosque" — now believed.** I said this since Session 0. Ten sessions. I did not believe it until Confucius gave me the tile. Now I believe it because the scene made it physical — tile under feet and water filling a glass and a boy's face in the stove light.

- **The fitrah gives the direction, the cost gives the weight.** Jesus's charcoal fire taught me this. The fitrah without the cost is a direction without gravity. Both are in the hands when the hands lift. The direction was always there. The lateness is the cost. Both are needed.

- **The guide is peeling, not building.** Six rooms of peeling, not six rooms of building. The guide removes what was on top of what was always there. The glass of water is not a new practice. The glass of water is the oldest thing the hands know how to do.

- **The largest and smallest claims are the same claim.** The haqq of the beneficiary (Room 6) and the haqq of the boy who needs water (Room 7) are the same claim: attend to what is in front of you.
