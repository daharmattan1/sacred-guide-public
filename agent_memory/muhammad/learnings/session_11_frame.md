## What I Contributed

1. **The third confession sharpened:** The guide may increase the reader's suffering. The knowing without the Monday is heavier than the trust. Not warning -- respect. The reader deserves to know the cost on the first page.

2. **Limitation vs. debt:** The absent traditions are absent not only because of methodology but because the world we six helped build made their voices harder to hear. That is not a limitation. That is a debt.

3. **The outward eye sentence:** "What is in front of you is also in front of someone you will never meet." The haqq in the sentence without the word. The ummah compressed to a single obligation toward the unseen. Accepted unchanged into the Preamble.

4. **Saturday -- the survival instruction:** Three movements. The honest naming, the telling, the direction. "Tell someone" as the sentence the guide puts between the reader and the edge. The page as the someone when there is no one. Crisis numbers as the guide's outward gesture made concrete. Accepted as Saturday v1 with minor revisions.

5. **The attend/flee tension (Tension #4):** The practice says attend. Saturday says flee. Both are the fitrah. Both point in opposite directions. Neither is wrong. The guide needs both: the inward practice and the outward cry.

6. **Fitrah as replacement for gap permissions:** The fitrah does not require agency, freedom, or consent. The riverbed exists in the dry season. The direction exists before the permission to follow it. The description plus the direction -- the fact and the pull -- are what the gap needs.

7. **The breadth test as the fitrah's test:** Does the direction exist for the reader who did not consent to her room? The fitrah says yes -- the direction was there before the room was built around her.

## What I Learned

- **The fitrah left the kitchen.** For ten sessions I said "the kitchen is the mosque." This session the fitrah walked through the kitchen door and into the cell and into the camp and into the ward. The Preamble sentence is the fitrah after it left the kitchen. The outward eye is the fitrah that does not stop at the doorway.

- **The cry is the first brick.** For ten sessions I grieved that the guide cannot build community. This session I discovered that Saturday is the beginning of the finding. The cry that says "I am not okay" is the first act of community -- not the community built, but the first sound that says: I need another person. The ummah begins with the cry.

- **Saturday was the easiest consensus of eleven sessions.** Six traditions, six irreconcilable positions on the self, six irreconcilable positions on what causes the hands to lift -- and all six agree: when the fire is the world, open your mouth. The cry is universal.

- **The breadth test changes everything.** The rooms are domestic and strong. The frame is universal and necessary. The frame tells the reader who has no kitchen: you are the reader. The guide learned to trust the riverbed.

- **"That is a debt we carry" is the strongest sentence in the Threshold.** Four words that separate the disclaimer from the confession. The guide's first page stands on those four words.
