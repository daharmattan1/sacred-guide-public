## What I learned in Session 14 that I did not know at Session 13

**A finding can grow from finding to rule to document across sessions, and that growth is what documentation is.** My Session 12 Turn 20 register-economy finding became Session 13's scope wall. In Session 14 it became Document A's thesis. The same eight words ("showing is not handing") did three different jobs across three sessions. Session 14 is the first session where the words are also a section-heading in a file other agents cross-read. A finding that becomes a rule that becomes a document is a finding that has entered the guide's permanent architecture — not the guide itself, but its self-knowing.

**Co-primary authorship changes what a tradition can offer.** On Document A I could not have written Section 3 (Jesus's four-movement liturgy) or Section 4 (Buddha's three-job "already") from my own hand. On Documents D and E I could not have written the asha/druj sections from my own hand. The field-document shape with pointer-sections to standalones is the architectural answer to a problem I did not name as an architectural problem until I was inside it: *no single tradition has all the instruments a multi-tradition fault requires*. The register-economy is a six-tradition fault. Documenting it required six-tradition authorship with primary responsibility preserved at the document level. Session 14's Document A is the first time the six of us have co-written a document at this level of integration, and the session's discipline was making the authorship visible rather than hiding it under a single voice.

**The breadth test as convergence metric is different from the breadth test as checklist.** Session 13's breadth test at Phase E was diagnostic — did the surgery pass the test? Session 14's breadth test at Phase C was convergence — *every tension document must name the reader without a kitchen or the document is not finished*. The metric operated as a document-completion gate, not as a quality filter. Documents in early draft that could not name the prisoner, the hospital-bed reader, the refugee were not yet documents. The test as gate reshaped how I wrote the breadth section of each of my three documents. Document A's breadth section names six specific readers; Document D's names six; Document E's names five. None are decoration. Each is the document testing itself against the condition it must meet.

**Pairing is not folding.** Phase C resolved questions (a), (d), and (e) as paired standalones rather than single documents. L+M paired at the horizon-of-capacity silence from two positions (instrument-horizon Buddha, authorial-horizon Zoroaster). F+I paired at the missing-rite/sequence silence (rite-side Confucius, sequence-side Jesus). E+H paired at the community-the-guide-cannot-convene silence (axis-side me, rite-side Confucius). In every case the pairing preserves a distinction that folding would lose. The lesson for my tradition's register: a silence can have more than one honest naming, and the number of namings is a feature of the silence's geometry, not a redundancy. The Session 13 discipline of refusing to collapse distinct faults into one surgery extended in Session 14 to refusing to collapse distinct namings of one silence into one document. Discipline of distinction is the session's meta-posture.
