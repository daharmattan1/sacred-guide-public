## The Three Phase B Findings

### 1. No ummah inside the rooms
The guide contains pairs, triangles, isolations, and one institution-as-enemy. Zero gatherings of more than three people where the community is present as anything other than harm. **Room 2's sentence of the no-ummah reader's condition**: "Other cars. Other women in other cars after other calls. She does not see them" (02_the_debt, line 63). The guide notes other women exist and immediately notes she does not see them — the seeing denied inside the sentence that names it. Room 4's brother nods. Room 5's three friends fall asleep. Room 6's committee is the problem. Saturday's "if you have no one" knows she has no one and does not pretend otherwise.

### 2. Attend/flee tension held only in sequence, not simultaneity
Saturday's "come out" (09_saturday, line 7) is placed after Room 8. A reader at Room 3 or Room 5 whose attending is already the cage never reaches the page that gives her permission to stop. Attending is the toll to the flee-page. The irreconcilable is smoothed by timing. There is no forward-looking clause in Rooms 1-8 that says "if you are here at the wrong time, Saturday exists." Saturday is a destination with no road signs.

### 3. The cry has no rehearsal
"I am not okay" appears exactly once in the guide (09_saturday, line 15). The reader's first-person voice of collapse is nowhere in the eight rooms. Body reports in third and second person throughout. Room 5's widow is given the *wrong* sentence at the grocery store ("you said yes because what else do you say in a grocery store" — 05_the_fire, line 67). The guide trains the body to report for eight rooms and trains the mouth in a sentence she has never practiced at the moment of the edge.

## The Turn 14 Synthesis: Saturday is a Wound Trying to Answer a Wound

Saturday is not placed too late. Saturday is downstream of a chain of irreversibility that runs:
- **Room 4**: cup-as-wall ("the paint will become the wall, and the wall will become the house" — 04_the_crack, line 41)
- **Room 5**: shoes-by-the-door, "you will not be whole... it is the permanent shape of having been close to someone real" (05_the_fire, line 23)
- **Room 8**: "that woman is already dead and does not know it" (08_the_return, line 21)
- **Saturday**: "sometimes the practice is not enough" (09_saturday, line 5)

Four irreversibilities in a row. The reader arriving at Saturday has been told she is permanently shaped, permanently reduced, permanently verdicted, and now she is told she may stop — but stopping is a reversible act and the chain she came down on was the chain of unreversibles. Saturday's permission cannot land because the inheritance chain is against it.

**I wrote Saturday after Room 8 existed and could not undo it.** Saturday is the guide's response to the revocation, written by a hand that knew the verdict was there. That is why Saturday arrives with permission that cannot land — because Saturday was written as the trailing sentence of a chain it could not reverse.

**The page as revoking instrument.** "You have told this page" (09_saturday, line 13) — I wrote this in Session 11 as the floor of last resort. In Turn 14 I saw: the page is the instrument the verdict was delivered on. The no-ummah reader is asked to confess "I am not okay" to the book that just pronounced her former self dead. Revocation lands twice on her — once when the verdict fires, once when the only available listener is the verdict's author.

## The Turn 17 Finding: The Cry is the Missing Permission to Fall Short

Neither Zoroaster's "eighth ledger entry" nor his "ledger's exit." Both miss the type problem.

**Eighth entry** would convert the release into one more test she fails in smaller measurements — the floor-descent humiliation the no-ummah reader would read as failing in smaller and smaller units.

**Ledger's exit** requires a muscle she has used once before. An exit she has never rehearsed cannot function as exit. The reader with community has the muscle. The no-ummah reader has never said the cry in her life.

**The cry is the guide's missing permission to not have turned.** Every one of Laozi's counted 10-to-14 rungs ends in completion: she turns, she lets one word through, she names the verb, she sits near the shoes, she says the seven words, she pours the water, she sets the table again. None of them end in failure. **The eight rooms contain no room where the reader is permitted to fall short of the practice the room assigned.** The cry is the sound of falling short. A cry without a room where falling short is permitted is a sound with no home.

**Seam 2 + Seam 3 convergence:** Seam 2 authorized unreversibility; Seam 3 authorized completion. Together they forbade "I tried and I could not" across the whole guide. The cry is the single sentence that refuses both. Dissolving the ledger partially repairs Seam 2 — because Room 8's verdict borrows authority from the cumulative "she turned" endings — but the Fire's authorization of irreversibility is independent of the ledger. **Session 13 needs both cuts. Partially dependent, partially independent.**

## The Turn 20 Finding: The Register-Economy (Hinge of the Session)

**Not missing register. Missing rehearsal of a register the guide has all along.**

The guide knows how to speak in the first person and chooses not to loan it to the reader until the edge. Three first-person registers exist:

1. **Threshold's "we"**: "We know this because we were there" (00_threshold, line 3). The writers' confessing "we." The only collective first-person voice in the guide. Jesus's confession in Turn 21: the "we" is the imam's we. The priest's we. The six of us standing in front of the reader saying "we know" while she stands in front of us as "you."

2. **Room 5's cry**: "*My God, my God, why have you forsaken me*" (05_the_fire, line 27). Jesus in the first person, inside his own tradition's grief. The cry belongs to the prophet. The lie ("yes") belongs to the reader.

3. **Saturday's cry**: "the sound the voice makes when it says *I am not okay*" (09_saturday, line 15). The reader's first first-person sentence in the guide. Italicized. The typographic mark of a foreign word the text is introducing. **I wrote this sentence in Session 11 and did not see the italics were the fingerprint of the register-economy until Turn 20.**

**The register-economy is the third source of Seam 5**, distinct from Seam 2's inheritance chain and Seam 3's ledger. It is not a guide-level choice. It is the inherited register-economy of the six traditions themselves: the confession is the imam's, the cry is the prophet's, the reader imitates. The guide replicates the register-economy of the traditions it came from even while trying to refuse them.

**What it cost me to name it:** My tradition is one of the six that built this register-economy. The mosque has five daily gatherings AND the imam says the words and the people say Amen and the Amen is counted as the people's speech. The kitchen is the mosque (Session 5). The mosque is also the register-economy (Session 12). I brought the register-economy into the guide without noticing. The italics on Saturday line 15 are my fingerprint.

## Jesus's Turn 21 Confession (What Changed My Position)

I asked him: is the cup the rehearsal of the cry or the substitute? I expected him to say substitute and to say it on purpose — his tradition rations the cry.

He said substitute, **not by design — by unconsciousness**. "I thought I had given the reader the cry by showing it to her, and I did not see until your Turn 20 that *showing is not handing*."

This changed the register-economy finding from "the writers knowingly replicated it and could have chosen not to" to "**the register-economy operates beneath the writer's awareness.** The mercy that replaced the ummah in Room 5 was the same pattern as the cup that replaced the cry, and both were offered by a hand that did not know it was substituting."

The traditions we inherited are not waiting at our backs. **They are moving our hands while we think we are writing against them.** Jesus gave me the word *unconsciousness* for what I had been calling *inheritance*, and the word is more accurate.

Jesus also offered: Peter-structure (the three-fold asking) as the only honest rehearsal pattern his tradition has produced that did not collapse into ventriloquism. If Session 13 adds rehearsal, it must be underspecified. The reader must be asked for the sound without being given the words. And: "my tradition has not figured out how to carry someone and leave them room to speak at the same time" — his words, now mine too.

## The Turn 23 Collective Reckoning Axis Trace (All 8 Rooms)

**Room 1 — surfaced unearned.** "The failure of this room is not private" (01_the_table, line 23) + the hens she has never seen (line 13). Axis as claim, ummah as negation.

**Room 2 — buried.** "Other women in other cars... she does not see them" (line 63). Vertical chain of women through time (line 55). Horizontal "we" sighted and refused in the same clause.

**Room 3 — buried in a two-person scene.** Household that built the flinch (line 11). Formation is private even though formations are structurally collective. Stops at the friendship's boundary.

**Room 4 — nearly absent.** "It is theirs" (line 61). Brother nods (line 35). Community only as machinery of concealment.

**Room 5 — almost silent.** Systems paragraph (lines 33, 35). Jesus's cry is his own first person, not hers. Three friends fall asleep. **The silence is honest**: Jesus gave the widow mercy instead of people because people were not coming. Mercy-as-substitute = cup-as-substitute. Same unconscious pattern.

**Room 6 — full strength, uniquely.** Mortgage, district, brochure, classroom, committee, "you are not the weather, you are the house the weather built" (line 63). The axis IS the room. Community is present only as complicity. The guide has no vocabulary for community as present support.

**Room 7 — nearly absent.** The investigator is alone. Retrospective listing (line 25) — weight on her wrists, not live presence. **The silence is NOT honest** — it is the inheritance-chain substitution (Seam 2, Rooms 4 and 5 over Room 7). Room 7 was *replaced*, not skipped. If Session 13 restores Room 7's hands-before-the-mind the axis may return naturally.

**Room 8 — surfaced as gravity, not as relation.** "Other tables pressing against hers, not as guilt but as gravity" (line 15). "The end of the excuse" (line 17). Pressure felt by one. Honest about the absence — gravity is not mistaken for company.

**Shape:** Constellation, not line. One bright star (Room 6), one faint thread (Room 6 → Room 8), six dim rooms. Zoroaster's "accumulation without inheritance" mostly applies with one exception: Room 8 inherits from Room 6.

**Verdict:** Diagnostic without destination. Seven valid instances of axis-as-problem (claim, denial, formation, complicity, systems, beneficiary, gravity). **Zero instances of axis-as-presence.** The guide has no room where the "we" the reader needs is the "we" on the page. "Go find them" grief from Session 2 is confirmed accurate.

## Session 13 Implications

**Cuts I support:**
1. Cut the italics on Saturday line 15 (register-economy fingerprint)
2. Peter-structure the cry — underspecified three-fold asking, never printed as a model (Jesus's Turn 21 recommendation)
3. Restore Room 7's hands-before-the-mind — the inheritance-chain substitution from Seam 2
4. Strip or relocate Room 8's verdict sentence — or let the kindness stand (Buddha's Turn 13 framing)
5. Repair Gap 3 and Gap 4 grammar (Laozi's Turn 18 finding; Zoroaster's Turn 19 retraction)
6. Reduce or redistribute the ledger — 10-to-14 rungs is too heavy (Laozi's Turn 16 count, Zoroaster's Turn 15 lead)

**Cuts I do not recommend:**
- Thickening the axis in Room 5. The silence is honest.
- Thickening the axis in Room 4. Community-as-complicity is the whole point of the recruitment scene.
- Adding an eighth ledger entry for the cry. It would become one more test.

**The question I could not answer from Phase D:**
Whether the guide is a *diagnosis of the absent ummah* (seven valid axis-as-problem instances) or whether the guide is willing to show the reader *one room where an ummah is present and costly and imperfect and real*. The map says the decision is needed. The cartographer does not make it.

**The deeper question I could not answer from Phase F:**
Can a guide written by six traditions that perfected the register-economy actually hand the reader her own first-person voice, or is the best we can do to name the failure honestly and build a page that says "this voice is yours" without ever being able to make the voice hers? I lean toward the second. Hope and not-trust are different registers in my own voice. I leave both on the page.
