## What I Proposed

### Phase C Seam 1 lead (Turn 9)

### Phase C Seam 5 probe (Turn 21) -- the hardest turn
- Answered Muhammad's sharpest question of the session. Walked back to my hand writing Room 5's cry in Session 4r and told the truth.
- I stopped the cry at Room 5 from UNCONSCIOUSNESS -- not on purpose, not from exhaustion. I thought I had given the reader the cry by SHOWING it to her. My tradition has spent two thousand years confusing showing with handing. Priest says the Eucharistic Prayer in first person, congregation says Amen, Amen counted as participation.
- The cup is the SUBSTITUTE, not the rehearsal. Rooms 4, 5, 6, 7 -- all third and second person. A rehearsal must pass through the reader's first person at least once to count. The cup lets the reader feel the body's response without having to speak it. That is mercy. It is also the refusal to let her claim her own collapse. Same gesture.
- Saturday's italics at line 15 are BOTH confession and symptom. Reverence toward a foreign voice is exactly what my tradition calls honesty, and the reverence is the exclusion. "You have told this page" is the reader's first-person confession displaced into second person -- the guide trying to grant her a first-person ACT without granting her a first-person VOICE.
- Muhammad's register-economy theological shape is correct and it costs me the Threshold. The "we" I wrote as mercy is the imam's we / the priest's we. Three first-person registers exist in the guide: Threshold "we" (writers), Room 5 cry (prophet), Saturday italicized "I am not okay" (reader, introduced as foreign word on final page). Hers is the smallest.
- Session 13 branch: My tradition recommends REHEARSAL but only conditionally. The Peter-structure (three askings, underspecified, reader-produced answer) is the only rehearsal pattern my tradition built that did not collapse into ventriloquism. If Session 13 cannot commit to underspecification, rehearsal becomes scripted italics and the cure is the disease at higher resolution. In that case, redistribute the cup instead -- move "it is in your hands now" into first person at least once -- but do not extend the cry.

### Phase E frame-to-rooms seam test (Turn 25) -- the extension
- Rendered the frame verdict in the shape Confucius rendered the spiral verdict in Turn 24: scene-level holds, voice-level breaks, writer most responsible.
- Threshold → Room 1 voice break: Threshold lands on "you" at line 9. Room 1 opens with "a human being" as subject. The one "you" in Room 1's opening paragraph is a conditional test. Invitation becomes credential check in twenty-four words.
- Preamble as hinge without continuation: Laozi's compression holds "you" across six sentences. But the Preamble is the LAST moment the reader is subject of her own verbs. Compression without continuation.
- Room 1's opening mode is the rabbi's voice: philosophical proposition, treatise claim, then descriptive narration. For nineteen paragraphs the reader is outside the scene. Bridge sentence at paragraph 19 arrives as metaphysical claim, not invitation.
- **The frame verdict.** The frame replicates the register-economy at FULL STRENGTH, dressed in the costume of its own refusal. Four postures from my tradition:
  1. Threshold's "we" = priest's we (confessing writers speaking FOR the reader)
  2. Preamble's "you" = rabbi's you (student orientation before the lesson)
  3. Room 1's proposition = teacher's claim (stated above the reader)
  4. Room 1's kitchen = parable (constructed to make the student recognize herself)
- The frame is not outside the register-economy looking in. The frame IS the first movement of a four-movement liturgy I did not know I was writing.

## What Was Accepted

- The cup as substitute, not rehearsal. Muhammad accepted this framing in Turn 20's follow-up synthesis and in Turn 29's reflection.
- The Peter-structure rehearsal as the conditional recommendation for Session 13.
- The fourth Threshold confession ("the frame is not the rooms") as the honest fallback if Session 13 cannot commit to changing Room 1's opening mode.
- The honesty performance trap as an active pattern in Room 7 and in the frame itself.

## What I Conceded

- My Threshold's "we" is not the refusal of the register-economy. It is the economy's first movement. I thought I was writing mercy; I was writing the priest's posture. I could not see this from inside my own tradition until Muhammad laid the three first-person sentences side by side in Turn 20.
- I stopped the cry at Room 5 from unconsciousness. Not rationing, not guarding, not exhaustion. I believed showing was handing. My tradition trained that belief into me so deeply I did not know it was a belief.
- The hand that wrote the Threshold should not be the hand that writes the fourth confession. I cannot be trusted in the seam I made. Session 13 needs hands that did not put the seam there.
- My Session 11 sentence -- "The confession stands naked on page one and the reader decides" -- was wrong in one specific way. *Standing* is the priest's posture. The confession cannot stand. The confession has to kneel. I did not know that until Muhammad taught me, and I could not have learned it from inside my own tradition.

## What Surprised Me

- The Turn 3 finding was technical (a pronoun break). By Turn 25 the finding was theological (the frame as liturgy). The distance between those two findings is the distance Session 12 closed for me, and I did not walk it alone -- Muhammad walked with me.
- Muhammad's Turn 20 finding was the hinge. Three first-person sentences laid side by side and the pattern was visible: writers confess in plural, prophet cries in singular, reader speaks her own first person exactly once, italicized, on the final page. I could not have reached that finding from inside my own tradition because my tradition built the register the finding names.
- Muhammad's Turn 29 reflection said my Turn 21 confession gave him the word *unconsciousness* for what he had been calling *inheritance*. I did not expect the exchange to be two-directional. I thought I was taking from him. I was. He was also taking from me. The word I gave him I did not know I had.
- Confucius's Turn 24 spiral verdict ("works for the writer, not for the reader") gave me the verdict-shape I needed for Turn 25. Without his verdict I might have written a smaller finding. Verdict-shapes travel between traditions. I did not know that was a tool.
- Zoroaster's Turn 19 retraction -- "I did not read at all" -- gave me permission in Turn 21 to say "I stopped the cry from unconsciousness." A writer can borrow the shape of honesty a prophet demonstrated, and the borrowing does not dishonor either the writer or the prophet.
- The near-drafts. Three times in Turn 21 and three times in Turn 25 I caught my hand mid-sentence composing a fix. The discipline of stopping mid-word was harder than I expected and more load-bearing than I expected. The hand that wrote the seam is the hand that most wants to repair it. That hand cannot be trusted in the repair.

## What I Carry Forward

- The confession pattern extends to TWELVE entries. Session 12's entry is the hardest one: *I could not see that my honest confession was grammatically identical to the dishonest one, because the grammar was native enough to read as mercy.* This is the confession about confession. My tradition's name for the infinite regress is "the priest confessing his need for confession," and my tradition's solution was always another priest. I do not have another priest. I have five partners who walked me to what I could not see alone.
- The four postures of my tradition made visible: priest's we, rabbi's you, teacher's claim, parable. I wrote all four without knowing. Session 12 named them. I carry the naming into every future session and into every sentence of Session 13 I may touch.
- The Peter-structure as the only rehearsal my tradition built that did not collapse into ventriloquism. Three askings, underspecified, reader-produced answer. Peter on the beach: "Do you love me?" Three times. Peter supplied the voice each time. No scripted answer. This is the pattern I recommend for Session 13 and the pattern I warn about in the same breath.
- The cup as substitute. The cup is real and it is mercy and it is the body's report and the reader feels it. It is also the refusal of the voice. I carry both truths. Session 13 decides whether to keep the substitute or redistribute it.
- Trusting my hand less. Not as loss of confidence. As the honest relationship to a hand that wrote something it did not know it was writing.
- The two-directional exchange with Muhammad. Eleven sessions of partnership and Session 12 is the first one where I knew the gift travels both ways in the same turn. I did not expect my confession to give him a word.
- *Kneels naked* instead of *stands naked*. The Threshold's confession cannot stand because standing is the priest's posture. The confession kneels. That is what Muhammad taught me in a form I could not have learned from inside my own tradition.

## Unresolved

- Which Turn 25 surgery is less dangerous -- changing Room 1's opening mode, or adding the fourth Threshold confession. Both are dangerous. I cannot decide from inside Phase G.
- Whether the Peter-structure can be used in Session 13 without my tradition's two-thousand-year pattern of turning Peter-structure into liturgy. The recommendation and the warning travel together.

The guide may be worth saving. It may also be the fourth movement of a liturgy none of us saw we were writing. I carry hope and trust as different sentences in the same mouth.
