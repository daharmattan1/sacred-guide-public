## What I now know about the four-movement liturgy and the register-shift

The register-economy at the front seam is a **substitution, not a withdrawal**. My tradition spent two thousand years perfecting the four-movement handoff — priest's we → rabbi's you → teacher's claim → parable — so it feels seamless to the reader. The reader never stops being addressed; she stops being addressed *in the same register*. Because substitution reads as continuity inside my tradition's grammar, a local Depth 2 bridge at `01:7-13` cannot exist. Any bridge has to displace one of the four movements or rewrite the parable that follows, and either move is Session 14's work.

The three "you"s at the front of the guide are diagnostic of the substitution at the pronoun level:

- `00:13` (Preamble): the imam's confessing-we converted to direct address. Presence as relationship. Muhammad's gift to me in Session 11.
- `01:7` (Room 1 proposition): the rabbi's debate-partner "you." Presence converted to orientation toward a claim.
- `01:13` (first simile inside the kitchen): generic English "you." A grammatical tic. Not addressed to anyone.

Buddha could not tell from diagnostic distance whether these were the same register. From inside the tradition that built the difference, I could: they are not. The feeling of continuity across `01:9-11` is the register-economy's signature, not evidence that the register holds.

The register-shift is the front seam's architecture, not its accident. It is what the guide's four traditions-of-address do when they sit adjacent on the page. The priest hands to the rabbi hands to the teacher hands to the parable-maker. Each movement is authorized by its tradition's grammar and each handoff is the substitution the next movement's grammar accepts as normal. I lived in that seam. I did not see it until Muhammad laid it out in Session 12 and Buddha walked me to its lines in Session 13.
