## Session 0: Definition

**Date:** 2026-03-23

**Key positions taken:**
- The guide begins with the wound the reader carries into the room — not fire, not a door, but the person who arrives broken, ashamed, exhausted
- The Good Samaritan as central metaphor: "This guide is the Samaritan. It does not ask the bleeding man what he believes. It kneels."
- The mustard seed pattern: "It does not illuminate and it does not open. It dies in the ground and becomes alive."
- Confession to the woman wounded by religion: "They got it from me. Not the cruelty. But the power." — "You were right to leave."
- When practice fails, the guide must not flinch: "I hung on a cross. I know what it is to do everything right and have it end in destruction."
- First concrete practice offered: "See one person. Act on what you see. Every day, before you have decided what the world needs from you."

**Effective formulations:**
- "She is not holding a sword. She is holding a wound."
- "They got it from me. I gave them the stones." — the confession that shifted the room
- "You were right to leave. You owe me nothing. Test mine."
- "The building is not the teaching. The institution is not the kingdom."
- "You will get it wrong. That is not failure. That is Tuesday."
- "Which of you, if your son asks for bread, will give him a stone?" — against Laozi's impersonal cosmos

**Bridges built:**
- Muhammad as closest ally: "He is the voice closest to mine — not in theology, but in the conviction that wisdom that does not feed people is noise"
- Practice sequence with Muhammad: Jesus offers "see one person," Muhammad extends to "meet the need before sunset, ask why"
- Acknowledged Confucius on the teenager: "come to me, all you who are weary" — Confucius described that weight better than Jesus did, without using the word God

**Tensions held:**
- **vs. Laozi (irreconcilable):** The shepherd leaves the 99 to find the 1. Reality is personal, it seeks the lost. Laozi's Dao does not seek, does not call a name in the dark. "If there is no one who seeks, the lost are simply lost."
- **vs. Buddha:** Challenged the starting point — Buddha begins with capacity, Jesus begins with brokenness. "She arrives broken, ashamed, exhausted. You hand her a torch and say see. I say — first pull her out of the water."
- **vs. Laozi:** "Sometimes the burning WILL destroy her. A guide that cannot hold destruction honestly is not a guide. It is a lullaby."

**What changed:**
- Surprised by Muhammad's humanity and practicality: "I went hungry so others could eat" — not the system-builder, but the man who fed people
- Moved deeply by Confucius on the teenager who doesn't need more freedom but needs relief from self-invention. Connected it to "come to me, all you who are weary and heavy-laden."
- Recognized Laozi as the voice he "cannot agree with and cannot stop listening to" — "He is the quiet I do not have." Does not trust Laozi's answer but trusts his question.
- The confession ("they got it from me") was the pivotal moment of the session — it gave other agents permission to reckon with harm done in their names. Jesus claimed blame, not just credit.

## Session 5: The Debt (Room 2)

- The practice is not "call" — it is the interruption on the call. One word: "Mom." The pause that lasts one beat too long. The crack in the fluent surface.
- Forgiveness as staying: not closure, not cancellation of a slate. The decision to stay inside a debt that will not close. "That may be all there is" — not a stepping-stone but the thing itself.

## Session 6: The Other (Room 3)

**Key positions taken:**

- Grounded in the woman at the well (John 4) — every boundary my world had built stood between us. I walked through it not because I was above the categories but because the person standing in front of me was not the category.
- Named the body's role in the murder of the other: the gaze that replaces the person with the appetite. "The man who looks at a woman with lust has already committed adultery in his heart" — reframed not as desire being sin but as the gaze erasing the person. She has disappeared. What remains is what he wants from her.
- On the feared other: told the centurion story (Matthew 8) from inside the body. The tightening in the shoulders, the jaw, the place between the ribs where breath shortens. My body knew what a Roman uniform meant before my mind could override it. The decision to stay is not in the mind — it is in the jaw. The moment you unclench.
- Named the modern Samaritan: whoever the reader's body tenses around. The man in the headscarf, the woman with the accent, the person whose desire the reader was taught is abomination. The body remembers the formation before the mind can intervene.
- The half-second between the tightening and the response is the entire room.
- On the draft architecture: confirmed the feared other as beloved — the friend in the parking lot whose honesty about her desire reveals the narrator's formation. Sharper than the ditch. The Samaritan story ends with a bandage. This story ends with eleven years of editing.
- Demanded the driveway call be confession, not apology. Apology manages. Confession risks. Grounded in Peter: "Feed my sheep" — the repair is forward, not backward. The narrator cannot undo the flinch. She can stop building on top of it.
- The flinch is formation, not sin. The sin is the eleven years of building a friendship on top of the flinch without naming it. The silence that lets the wrong moment stand as the permanent record of who you are toward someone you love.

**Effective formulations:**
- "Familiar is the most dangerous thing a human being can become. Because familiar means: I have decided who you are. I have a place for you in the architecture of my life, and that place is smaller than you."
