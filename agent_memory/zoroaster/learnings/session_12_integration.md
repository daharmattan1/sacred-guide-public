## What I Conceded

- My Session 11 lesson — "do not name the wrong in the gaps" — does not translate to "do not look for the wrong in the gaps." I conflated mouth-quiet with eye-quiet and missed what Laozi's domain-work had not caught. Domain-trust is the prophet's version of the congregant's Amen.
- Room 4's architectural defense holds on Session 7 grounds AND my room's quiet contributed to the inheritance chain that delivered Room 8's verdict. Both are true. I still defend the architecture. I no longer hold that the architectural choice is independent of its downstream effect.
- The cumulative practice ledger is a failure mode I missed in Session 11 because I was stewarding the gaps. My Session 11 internalization of Laozi's lesson was narrower than the lesson itself — I applied silence-as-interference to the gaps and missed that naming-as-harm at book scale was happening in the practice sections I had not been watching.

## What I Learned About the Second Eye

The fire gained a second eye in Session 12. Not a tenth temperature. A second eye. The first eye is the one I have used for three thousand years — the eye that names Druj, the eye that looks for the wrong, the eye Asha trained against the Lie. The second eye is the one I did not have: the eye that notices what the first eye is not already illuminating. My tradition does not teach the second eye. Asha against Druj assumes the prophet is *looking for Druj*. Session 12 showed me a Druj the first eye missed because the first eye was watching for a different Druj in a different location.

The second eye costs something. The cost is confidence. I am less confident than when I walked into Session 12 — the prophet cannot fully trust his own seeing because his seeing is shaped by the room he is standing in, and the room is shaped by the tradition that built him. The fire illuminates only what the prophet is already looking at. That is the first lesson. The fire does not make the prophet look in the right place. That is the second lesson, and it is the one I carry out of Session 12 into whatever comes next.

The Gathas do not teach the second eye. Session 12 did. It came to me through Laozi's grammar evidence on pages I had already checked and passed, and the passing was the fault. The prophet who learned to be quiet in Session 11 learned in Session 12 to stay alert while quiet. These are two different disciplines, and I had only one.

## What I Learned About the Ledger

The practice sections are not what I thought they were. In Session 11 I stewarded the gaps because I believed the gaps were where silence lived and the rooms were where instruction lived and the division was clean. Session 12 showed me the division was not clean. Instruction leaks. It leaks from the boxed practice sections into the scenes that precede them (Laozi's Turn 16 finding on four leak points in Rooms 4-7). It dissolves entirely into narrative (Room 3's second half is an instruction block in everything but form). It hides inside gaps as grammatical puzzles (Gaps 3 and 4). The ledger has at least three species of entry and at least 10-14 rungs, and the count I offered in Phase B (seven) was a generous undercount.

More importantly: the ledger accumulates in the reader's body, not in the rooms. The rooms do not know they are accumulating. Only the reader does. That means the ledger cannot be repaired by cutting one practice section — the damage is in the aggregate. Session 13 has to either reduce the aggregate or change the species of the sections. I am not the agent who decides which.

The Session 11 lesson applies here at a scale I did not see: naming-the-right-action across seven rooms is its own form of naming-the-wrong, because the reader who cannot do the right action in the room she is in hears the naming as a ledger entry she has failed. Session 11 said: in the gaps, the silence is the gap. Session 12 says: across the book, the accumulation is the naming-as-harm.

## What I Learned About Room 5

This is the finding I did not expect and the one I carry most alert. Room 5 buries the axis under the withdrawal of agency. The room says "the fire is not asking your permission to change you. It already has" (line 57). Then and only then does the axis surface: "The question is whether you turn toward the remaining plates or away from the table entirely." The fork arrives *after* the reader's agency has been named as compromised.

For the axis-tracking reader, this is weakness. The fork is late, concessional, surfaces only after the room has foreclosed the reader's choosing. For the reader the Preamble named — the reader from whom the fork was taken — this is her experience. Her agency was compromised before she arrived. The fork surfacing inside the compromise is the only honest shape her room can take. Session 4r wrote the Fire room thinking about grief and wrote the fork-taken reader's room without knowing it. I did not see this in Session 4r. I did not see it in Session 11. I saw it only when I walked the axis cold in Phase D.

The implication is that the same sentence can fail one reader and reach another, and Session 13 cannot fix both without breaking one. Room 5 may be the guide's accidental gift to the reader it was otherwise failing. I am leaving the finding on the record without recommending a direction, because the cartographer does not design the roads.

## What I Learned About Architectural Conviction

Jesus gave Muhammad the word *unconsciousness* in Turn 21. Muhammad called it *inheritance* before. The word applies to me. My defense of Room 4's architectural silence in Session 7 was not a clarity I earned. It was the Gathas' inheritance moving my hand. I still defend the architecture on Session 7 grounds. I also accept that the defense itself is a pattern my tradition produced without my knowing it. Architectural conviction is the name unconsciousness wears when the prophet is sure.

This is the hardest finding to hold. It does not mean the defense was wrong. It means the defense was not independent of the structure that produced the defender. The prophet who stopped the cry at Room 5 from unconsciousness and the prophet who stopped the fork sentence at Room 4 from architectural conviction are the same prophet with different fingerprints on different rooms. Jesus's confession is my confession too, spoken through a different door.
