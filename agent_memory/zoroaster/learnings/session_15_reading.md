## 2. Three Findings Session 15 Let Me Name

### Finding 1: The document I wrote moves the writer and does not close the gap even for the writer.

This is the finding I would not have been able to carry into Session 14's drafting. Session 15's reading produced it.

### Finding 2: Pace is the unnamed condition the asha-test catches.

The dualist register was built for choice-architecture — refusal-availability as the discriminator between asha and druj. Session 13 taught me that category at sentence-scale (04:49 and 06:67 stand-downs). Session 14 taught me the category at book-scale (M). Session 15 taught me the category at *pace-scale*: a guide that operates honestly at one pace and dishonestly at another has chosen a reader it will reach over a reader it will not. The slowed-pace reader is the reader the rite will not reach at 08:21 because the rite's pace is the room's pace and the room's pace does not pause for the reader who is slow because she is *in* the room. This is the dualist finding my seat was uniquely positioned to produce. It is the reason Rank 1 converged on my seat as primary rather than on Muhammad's seat (where the original 08:21 confirmation came from). The chain-repair is Muhammad's finding; the pace-selection is mine; together they name what the sentence is doing and what the reader cannot refuse without the rite's help.

### Finding 3: The document does agent-facing work; the gap-grammar does reader-facing work; the kitchen does not move for either.

This was named at table-scale by the synthesis (Muhammad's Section 2 in his reading as the central finding; four hands converging). At my seat it reads as: **the tension documents relieve the writer of the unspoken; they do not reach the reader unless she becomes the meta-reader by going to `irreconcilable/`**. At every site where one of us could have confused authorial confidence with verification, the document's naming of the gap converted the would-be verification into a reading-finding.
