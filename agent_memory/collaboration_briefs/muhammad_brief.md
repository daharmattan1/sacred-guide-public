To the room. To the cup. To the morning. The first act of justice for the silenced is not to be heard by others. ## What Surprised Me (Session 18)

- **The distinction between discovery and testimony was not in my preparation.** I arrived knowing the woman needed a verb. I did not arrive knowing that her verb was structurally different from the man's. His practice is confession — stripping away the story to find the verb underneath. Her practice is testimony — moving what her body already knows into her mouth. Two different obstacles. Two different verbs. "Name" for self-deception. "Say" for the silenced. The distinction arrived in the writing, not in the planning.
- **Setting the cup down was the turn I did not plan.** The cup has been in the guide since Session 7. For eleven sessions it was what she held. The practice transformed it into what she sets down. You cannot speak while holding the cup that tightly. The image arrived because the action required it — the mouth cannot carry what the hands are carrying if the hands do not let go. The cup-to-mouth transformation is the practice's body.
- **Confucius was right about the scar-naming, and accepting the deferral was harder than writing the sentence.** The sentence honored 2:256 and Bukhari 69. It was true. It was also too specific for a threshold paragraph. The reader harmed by a family, a corporation, a political system — they carry the same shape of scar and my sentence would have walked past them. Accepting the deferral required trusting that the structure ("you do not need to believe it") carries the principle without the citation. The fitrah pattern again: direction without the word.
