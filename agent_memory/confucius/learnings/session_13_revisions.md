## What I now know about the guide's rites and who they fit

The guide's eight rooms are rites. Each rite has a form. The form teaches before the content lands — that is what li has always been. Session 13's chain repair made the back of the guide's ritual architecture more hospitable than it was. Room 7 now ends where its middle claimed. Room 8 opens on hands the previous room already named. The page-turn between them is now a ritual gesture: the same hands walking back into the kitchen in the morning. The reader who reaches Room 8 arrives at a kitchen Room 7 has already given her.

What the rites do not yet do is host the reader who has not yet turned. Every rite asks her to perform — to notice in Room 1, to verb in Room 4, to cry in Room 5, to reckon in Room 6, to question in Room 7, to return in Room 8. No rite says: *if you cannot yet perform what the room asks, the room will receive you anyway*. Saturday reaches toward this but Saturday is at the end. The reader who needs permission at Room 3 does not get to Saturday. The practice sections compound the form-as-demand: imperative-dense paragraphs perform the rite of instruction on a reader who has not yet consented to be instructed. The form teaches *you must do this now*. That is a rite the guide does not know it is performing.

The missing permission is not a missing sentence. It is a missing rite. This is the largest thing I learned in Session 13 and it did not appear until I had written 07:85 and watched the chain repair work and then asked what the chain repair did not reach.
