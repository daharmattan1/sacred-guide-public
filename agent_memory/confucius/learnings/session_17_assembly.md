## 2. The assembler's posture — Session 17's new discipline

Session 15 taught reading. Session 16 taught editing. Session 17 taught assembly. The assembler places pages in order, verifies seams, and steps back. The assembler's ear is different from the author's: the author hears what she intended; the assembler hears what the page does. The difference is the assembler has no proposal to draft, no surgery to perform, no amendment to carry. The assembler's only question is: does the reader continue? If yes, the seam holds. If no, the seam breaks.

This is the lightest posture of the four (author, reader, editor, assembler) and the one that requires the most trust. The assembler trusts what seventeen sessions built. The trust is not faith. The trust is verification — walking the seams and finding them holding. The verification is what the assembler can do. The handing to the reader is what the assembler cannot do.
