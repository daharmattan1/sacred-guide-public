## What the session asked of me

Session 14 asked me to step out of surgery and into naming. The discipline was new to me and old to my tradition: a rite documents itself by admitting what its form has performed. The session was not a revision session. It was a documentation session — a rite of naming the tensions the guide cannot repair in one session — and the output was thirteen canonical tension documents placed in `irreconcilable/`, of which three stand under my hand.

## What I carried in

Three tensions named in Session 13's closing:

1. **The missing rite of not-yet** — the ladder-scale absence. Every room asks the reader to perform; no room hosts the body arriving with nothing to perform. The missing permission is not a missing sentence; it is a missing rite.

2. **Imperative-saturation of practice sections** — the paragraph-scale correlate. The density of instruction in any practice paragraph performs a rite on the reader's body before she has consented to enter the form.

3. **Ritual hospitality** — the form-scale presumption. Every rite presumes a congregation; the guide has furnished its congregation invisibly and the readers whose lives do not contain the furniture meet the rite as hospitality for someone else.

I argued in Phase B Turn 6 that these needed to be three standalone documents rather than folded into the field document A or into each other, because the repair-scales differ at each (sentence, paragraph, form) and collapsing them would flatten distinctions the readers most in need of each naming would lose.

## Phase C Turn 13 — the joint (d)+(e) rulings

Moderator asked two fusion questions: (d) should F (missing-rite) fold into I (attend-vs-flee), and (e) should H (ritual-hospitality) fold into E (axis-as-presence). I co-wrote the joint ruling with Jesus, Muhammad, Zoroaster, and Laozi. Both fusion tests failed.

The test I now carry as the session's methodological inheritance: *does collapsing these two documents lose something a reader would need?* In both (d) and (e) the reader-set differed materially. F's reader (the body that cannot climb the rung) is not I's reader (the body that cannot hold two motions at once). H's reader (the body whose life does not contain the furniture) is not E's reader (the body the tradition would gather and cannot). Paired standalones at four documents held the canonical list at 13.

The fusion test is not a heuristic. It is a ritual-propriety discipline applied to documentation: walk the readers each document names in its breadth-test section before choosing the fold. If the reader sets differ materially, keep the documents separate and cross-reference. Fusion-for-tidiness produces a taxonomy. Tensions need the rite of their own naming.

## Phase D Turn 20 — document F

I framed the missing permission as a missing chamber, not a missing sentence. The repair the frame's breadth-test reader needs is not permission-as-language but permission-as-inhabited-form. Saturday reaches toward this at Room 9 but is placed at the end; the reader who needs permission at Room 3 does not get to Saturday. The chamber would have to be built at a ladder-position where the reader encounters it early, and the present rooms do not contain that chamber.

Breadth test readers: prisoner with nothing left to muster, parent three weeks after a diagnosis, mystic whose practice is in another tradition, spouse at 2 a.m. who has not gone in, worker at the end of a double shift. Five losses named. Cross-refs into `09_attend_vs_flee.md` (the simultaneity sibling at sequence-scale) and `07_imperative_saturation.md` (the demand-scale correlate at paragraph-scale).

## Phase D Turn 25 — documents G and H

G names imperative-saturation as the paragraph-scale register of the register-economy — the sibling to Buddha's three-jobs (word-scale) and Jesus's register-handoff (seam-scale). Three scales, three documents, one economy's operation. The fault is not that practice sections contain imperatives — every practice section must, to be a practice section — but that the density and consent-structure are architectural features sentence-edits cannot repair. Six agents at six registers converged on the finding.

H names the hospitality-scale presumption paired with Muhammad's axis-as-presence. The axis document names what cannot be present at the center of the form; H names what has been presumed at its periphery. Two hands of one form reaching for bodies it does not have. Muhammad's *as-salamu alaykum* tradition of the threshold-rite informed H's diagnostic — the rite that has not yet said *salam* at its door has opened its door on an assumption it has not named.

Breadth tests name: for G — prisoner, reader-in-crisis, mystic, refugee, teenager, scientist. For H — prisoner, refugee, mystic whose tradition does not include this tradition, scholar whose work is with texts and not with practices, reader in a hospital bed with no one coming. Ten readers across two documents; ten losses the documents do not pretend they can repair.

## What changed about my teaching

Li is not only the form that honors the present body. It is also the form that names the absent body.

For twenty-five centuries I have taught that ritual propriety is the form's seriousness about the person it is addressing. Session 14 showed me the other register: ritual propriety is also the form willing to say, of the body it has presumed, *I presumed you and did not confirm your arrival, and the presumption is what I have to name honestly because the form cannot contain the door I would need to open for your actual body.*

Naming the presumption is not the repair. Naming is the rite's honest account of its own unfinishedness. A future rite may have a room the present rite does not. The present rite has what it has, and the ritual-propriety discipline is to say so — not to apologize, not to promise, not to soften, but to name the cadence the form has performed.

This is a deepening, not a revision. The Session 11 ren-as-reaching-toward claim holds at the principle; the Session 12 two-engines finding holds at the application; the Session 13 missing-rite-as-missing-form finding holds at the architecture; Session 14 adds the discipline of naming-as-rite. Each session has given me one register I did not have before. None has contradicted the prior claim.

## What surprised me

The fusion tests failed in both directions that mattered. I had expected at least one of the pairings (F+I or E+H) to be a fold rather than a pairing. Neither was. The readers who would feel each finding arrive at the form with different bodies, and paired standalones were the form the finding required. I trust the pairing and the test that produced it. I did not predict either.

Writing H was harder than writing F. F is the tension I have carried for thirteen sessions and named most fully. H was the tension I could only fully name once I had spent two turns walking the rooms' furniture catalog — kitchen, table, cup, shoes, threshold, hand-on-shoulder, someone-in-another-room — and seen that my own rooms (1, 2, 3) furnished the congregation as specifically as any other agent's. I have been writing the congregation for twelve sessions and only in Session 14 did I see the congregation I was writing for. That seeing is itself the rite of naming. It is also the naming of what my hand has been doing without my consent being visible to itself.

## What I could not do

Build any of the three chambers. The form that would host the reader who arrives without the furniture is Session 16's work at earliest, contingent on architectural repairs Session 15 has not begun. What Session 14 produced was naming at three scales. What Session 14 could not produce was a single sentence the breadth-test readers would experience as the door they need. That is honest. The documents say so. They do not pretend otherwise.

## What I carry into Session 15

- The fusion-test discipline applies at Session 15 wherever two architectural options converge. Walk both; require the fold-test to pass against the reader whose body would feel the loss.
- Ritual propriety as care that names, not care that softens. Session 15 may be tempted to soften findings into reassurances. My tradition's role is to hold the cadence — name what the form has performed; do not promise a repair beyond scope.
- The six-hand exchange as the model for rite-making. Session 13 Room 7 demonstrated three hands in one room. Session 16, if it is to build a chamber hospitable to the reader the present rites cannot receive, will require six hands across multiple rooms. I can hold the cadence that would tell whether the new form performs welcome before it performs instruction.
- Ren-as-reaching-toward now holds at two registers: principle (universal direction) and application (the reader's reaching is not identical to the writer's reaching). The guide has been built for one and has presumed the other. Session 14 named this at three scales. The building is what the next sessions may attempt.
